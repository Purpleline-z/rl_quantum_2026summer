## 2. What was asked, and the protocol extension
In real use the lab scores *new frames of a run while it is being grown*: their images exist (unlabelled) and the past of the run is labelled. All earlier protocols (a) keep held-out images out of the graph (identity rule: "inductive attachment") and (b) split at random, so test images have near-duplicate training frames on both sides in time. This study defines and develops the deployment-faithful protocol (stated as a protocol extension in `PREREGISTRATION_TD0/TD1`):

* **Transductive**: the held-out images enter the graph as unlabelled nodes (features only, never labels; outer-test ideal images stay excluded). Node sets (forward): `ind` = past frames + ideal images (the future does not exist yet), `trans_test` = ind + the images being scored, `trans_win` = every frame that exists when the window is scored (causal), `trans_fut` = every frame of the data, `std` = the repository's graph (future non-test frames are nodes, test images are not). On the standard split all transductive sets coincide (`trans` = std + held-out images).
* **Forward-in-time**: runs ordered by date, an image's time = (run, frame), a group's time = that of its later image; cuts at 12, 24, 40, 54, 70, 90 calendar-ordered groups (two per run, ±2 jitter by seed); labelled = the past (identity rule applied); test windows FA = next 40 groups, FB = following 34 groups (the test sizes of Splits A and B), decisive judgments only.

{{TABLE:data_facts}}

**Which groups can be assigned a time:** all 168 (every image of every group is a trajectory frame); none cannot. 104 of 168 groups are cross-run pairs; the calendar order puts such a group at its later run, so a cut inside the last run (411A) has all of 204A and 206A in the past, a cut inside 204A has almost nothing but 204A–204A pairs. The identity rule (drop past groups that share an image with a test group) removes a large share of the past at the early cuts (e.g. 14 → 8 groups at the first cut): this is a conservative choice.

**Number of test judgments (honest count):** per (seed, cut, window) the forward windows contain 44–81 decisive judgments (see the confirmation tables); windows of different cuts and seeds overlap heavily (the same 168 groups are re-used), so the effective number of independent test judgments is far smaller than 35 seeds × 6 cuts × 60. The seed-level tests below therefore describe training variability only; the test-group bootstrap is the interval that speaks to new groups.

## 3. Methods tried (≤ 10 variants carried to the screen; every variant is in the log)
Laplacian with test frames as nodes (`L:`), test-time smoothing of the final scores over the graph without retraining (`SP:`), iterative co-training on pseudo-labelled pairs of unlabelled future frames (`CT:`), anchor posteriors (label spreading of the ideal-image classes) propagated to the future frames and used as a ranking loss (`AQ:`), time-weighted loss emphasising recent frames (`TW:`); controls: shuffled graph (`:shuf`), targeted shuffle of the held-out nodes only (`:tshuf`), graph-free consistency regulariser on the same nodes (`C:`), inductive versions (`L:ind`, `AQ:ind`), tuned graph-free `G0` = `aw8_all_st2lr0.5`. **Reading of the development screens (observations, not evidence).** (i) The decomposition BASE → STD → trans: letting the *future frames* into the graph (STD) gives the far window +0.012 AUC and nothing in the near window; adding the scored frames themselves (`trans_test`) is what helps the near window; the causal `trans_win` set gives the largest far-window gain. (ii) The graph-free consistency control on the same nodes gets about 60 % of the far-window AUC gain but loses accuracy, so the graph's share is mainly accuracy. (iii) Test-time propagation `SP` does nothing when the head was trained with the Laplacian (in-graph smoothing ±0.002 AUC); smoothing test images toward their nearest *past* nodes gains far-window AUC (+0.009) but loses accuracy (−0.007…−0.012). (iv) Recency weights `TW` are null (±0.002). (v) Pseudo-label co-training `CT` helps the far window slightly and hurts the near window. (vi) Shuffled graphs are very harmful (−0.13 AUC), so "beats the shuffled control" is a weak statement; hence the targeted control, the graph-free control, and the inductive references. (vii) Focus on the scored frames and λ = 10/30 do not give a consistent gain.

**Mechanism audit (descriptive, development seeds, `L:trans_win` vs BASE, per judgment pooled over cuts and seeds):** the gain is where the scored images are far from every past node (top quartile, mean cosine distance 0.10–0.15: accuracy +0.06…+0.07), not where near-duplicates of the past exist (near window: nearest three quartiles −0.008…−0.027 accuracy):

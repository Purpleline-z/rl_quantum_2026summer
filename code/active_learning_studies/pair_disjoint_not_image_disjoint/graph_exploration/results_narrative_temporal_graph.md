# Temporal edges for the Laplacian learner (branch `claude/temporal-graph-20261013`)

Every table is generated by `tg_report.py` / `tg_tables.py` from the result JSONs (`new_directions/results/tg/*`); the text is hand-written. Same 168 labelled pair groups as all earlier work (re-split with new seeds, **not new data**): p-values are optimistic; only the pre-registered confirmation (seeds 9250–9284, frozen in `PREREGISTRATION_TG1.md` before any of those cells existed) counts as evidence. Development seeds: 9200–9219. Baseline throughout: the incumbent `lap3_k10_st4` (= `aw8_all` + Laplacian regulariser λ = 3 on the k = 10 cosine kNN graph of unlabelled images + 4× head steps).

## 1. Answer
**No. Adding temporal edges (adjacent frames of one growth run) to the Laplacian learner does not improve it, and the pre-registered confirmation is clear about that.**

**Pre-registered confirmations (seeds 9250–9284, splits A and B, run once; frozen before any confirmation cell):**
- None of the three frozen candidates (decayed chain ±5 at weight 3 and 0.3; second-difference/curvature penalty) is confirmed. There is no Holm-significant test among 12 (smallest raw p = 0.066, smallest Holm-adjusted p = 0.79). The gains over `lap3_k10_st4` are +0.0004…+0.0018 AUC (decayed chain), −0.0005…−0.0002 (gentle chain) and +0.0016…+0.0017 (curvature), accuracy −0.004…+0.0015; all 95 % intervals contain 0 and for every candidate at least one of the four required gains is not positive.
- The candidates beat their shuffled-time controls by +0.011…+0.12 AUC, which is a weak statement (the shuffle links feature-distant frames and wrecks the learner); it only shows the temporal edges are not harmful noise.
- **Anchor-far endpoint: no gain** (far AUC −0.019…+0.004). The only nominal positives are *anchor-near* AUC gains of +0.003…+0.004 (uncorrected, secondary), and the curvature candidate pays for its near gain with worse log-loss (−0.012…−0.0135) and HTR accuracy (≈ −0.01).
- **Red-team time-blocked split (secondary): null** (AUC differences 0.0000 / +0.0002 / −0.0002 over 35 contexts); the expectation that temporal edges help most there is not supported.

**Development observations (20 seeds, 18 variants; selection effects apply — not evidence):** most variants are null or harmful; 84–94 % of the chain edges are already kNN edges, so the temporal term mostly re-weights existing edges; the temporal edges that the feature graph does not contain link feature-distant frames and smoothing across them costs AUC, log-loss and the anchor-far endpoint (my reading, untested: they sit next to changes of surface state); a time-only graph (−0.02 AUC) and a temperature-bin graph (−0.16 AUC) are clearly worse; removing the ≈ 4 % cross-run / far-in-time kNN edges costs ≈ 0.006–0.007 AUC while up-weighting them does nothing. Temperature is **not** monotone in time in these runs (heating then cooling; Spearman 0.30–0.63 with the frame index), so the physics hypothesis was neither needed nor supported.

**Exploratory (spare seeds, not pre-registered):** the one post-hoc hint from the confirmation (far accuracy of the gentle chain) did not replicate; the incumbent's own gain is not concentrated in time-adjacent test judgments but, as the red-team branch found, in anchor-near ones.

**Caveats (read with the numbers):** the data are 284 frames of three runs and test images are near-duplicates of training frames; "time-blocked" in the red-team split is not fully buffered (the partner image of a cross-block test group lies outside the held-out blocks; 55–70 % of graph nodes are within 25 frames of some held-out image), and the strict 25-frame rule removes ≈ 98 % of the temporal edges in the standard splits, so the strict variant is the baseline there. Same 168 pair groups throughout; a null at this effect size means "no gain larger than ≈ ±0.004 AUC detectable with 35 seeds on frozen SimCLR features", not "time carries no information".

## 2. What was asked, and what is new relative to the parallel branches
The kNN graph is built from frozen SimCLR features only. Adjacent frames of one growth run (file name `<index>_<run>_<temperature>C_<frame>.bmp`) are a free, label-free source of structure the graph ignores; earlier temporal attempts (contrastive adapter, filename metadata fusion) were negative but used other mechanisms. This study adds *temporal edges to the Laplacian term* and asks whether the held-out preference endpoint improves. The parallel branches did not test this: `graph-mechanism` studied graph quality / constructions of the *feature* graph and RT13-off (no temporal edges); `graph-endpoint` studied the classification endpoint, the amount of unlabelled data and harsh splits (one of them a "forward in time" split, where the Laplacian learner's AUC gain over `aw8_all` was small and the shuffled-graph controls gained more) — no temporal edges inside the learner. The red-team branch's `RESULTS_redteam.md` was not available when this study ran (only its tools `rt_*.py`, which are reused: anchor-distance definition `rt_a1b.py`, blocked split `rt_blocked.py`).

**Red-team caveats respected.** The data are 284 frames of three growth runs; test images are almost always near-duplicates of training frames. Every study report below therefore gives, next to the standard endpoint, an **anchor-far endpoint** (both test images > 0.03 cosine distance from every typed ideal-image anchor; ≈ 30 % of the test judgments) and its complement (anchor-near), and says so when a gain exists only in the near stratum. The time-blocked split (`rt_blocked.py`) is a secondary endpoint.

## 3. Facts about the data that the temporal idea relies on
**Temperature is not monotone in time.** The three runs heat up and cool down (first → last temperature 933 → 934, 165 → 165, 180 → 278 °C); the Spearman correlation between frame index and temperature is only 0.30–0.63. The physics team's hypothesis (monotone evolution with temperature) is therefore not used anywhere as an assumption: temperature only enters edge weights (`tg_dec5`) and bins (`tg_tbin`), both as hypotheses to be tested. Consecutive indices are consecutive camera frames (median gap 1 frame), so "25 frames" in the red-team buffer ≈ 25 indices.

{{TABLE:run_stats}}

**Temporal neighbours are almost always feature neighbours already.** 84–94 % of the chain edges (±1…±5 indices) are edges of the k = 10 kNN graph, and temporal neighbours that are *not* kNN edges are the feature-distant ones (median cosine distance 0.012, 24 % above 0.05): they sit where the image changes fast. Temperature-bin partners (any run, same 20 °C bin) are essentially unrelated images (median cosine distance 0.49). The within-run shuffled control is accordingly very harsh (median distance 0.26), the local shuffle milder (0.008), random kNN edges are as close as real ones but carry no time information.

{{TABLE:graph_stats}}

**The strict time-buffer rule removes almost all temporal edges in the standard splits.** With the red-team rule ("no node within 25 frames of a held-out image in the same run") only 34 of 1766 chain ±2 edges survive in an example context, because the held-out images (validation + test: 120 in split A, 65 in split B) are spread over all three runs (83–98 % of the trajectory nodes lie within 25 frames of some held-out image in the three contexts checked); the strict variant of any temporal candidate is therefore the baseline in the standard splits and was not made a candidate. In the red-team blocked split the rule holds for test images inside the held-out blocks (unit-tested), **but not for the partner image of a test group with one image in a block and one outside**: 55–70 % of the graph nodes of a blocked context are within 25 frames of some held-out image. The blocked split therefore does not give a clean "time-buffered" test; the `inb_*` stratum (both test images inside held-out blocks) is the part where the buffer fully holds.

### 4.6 What development suggests (hypotheses, not findings)
- **Temporal adjacency is not label identity.** Temporal edges that are also feature edges add nothing the kNN term does not already give; the temporal edges that the feature graph does *not* contain (`tg_novel5`) link frames that look different, and smoothing across them costs AUC (−0.007…−0.011), log-loss (−0.055) and the anchor-far endpoint (−0.04…−0.07 accuracy). My reading (untested): these are frames next to a change of surface state, where the score *should* jump.
- **A graph built from time alone loses what the feature graph provides** (`tg_only2` −0.02 AUC, `tg_tbin` −0.16): the gain of the Laplacian learner needs feature-coherent edges (consistent with the dose-response of the graph-mechanism branch).
- **The cross-run / far-in-time kNN edges are worth keeping** (removing them costs ≈ 0.006–0.007 AUC), but weighting them more does not help.
- **Anchor-far is where temporal terms hurt first** (negative far gains for most variants, even when the standard AUC is ≈ 0); a standard-endpoint gain without a far gain would therefore be an anchor-near effect.

## 5. Pre-registered confirmation (seeds 9250–9284, splits A and B, one draw, run once; frozen in `PREREGISTRATION_TG1.md`)
**Verdict: not confirmed — for all three frozen candidates**, as stated in advance. The main family is Holm-corrected over 12 tests; the smallest raw p is 0.066 (decayed chain ±5, AUC, split A) and the smallest Holm-adjusted p is 0.79. The 95 % intervals of all twelve gains contain 0; the gains are +0.0004…+0.0018 AUC (`tg_dec5`), −0.0005…−0.0002 AUC (`tg_dec5_mu0.3`), +0.0016…+0.0017 AUC (`tg_curv`) and between −0.004 and +0.0015 in accuracy; for every candidate at least one of the four gains is negative or ≈ 0, so the first requirement of the success rule already fails. The development winner (`tg_dec5`, +0.002 AUC over 20 development seeds) behaves exactly like a winner's-curse selection: +0.0018 / +0.0004 in confirmation.

*Controls.* Every candidate beats its shuffled-time control by a wide margin (AUC +0.011…+0.12), but this is a weak statement: random within-run pairs are feature-distant, and such edges destroy the learner (the graph-mechanism branch found the same for random long-range edges); it shows that the temporal edges are not *harmful noise*, not that they carry useful temporal information. The success rule's three requirements are reported separately in the verdict table, so the reader can see which one fails (the first, and the Holm count).

*Anchor-far / anchor-near (secondary, uncorrected, not in any rule).* There is no anchor-far gain: far AUC −0.004 / −0.004 (`tg_dec5`), −0.003 / +0.004 (`tg_dec5_mu0.3`), −0.019 / −0.007 (`tg_curv`), far accuracy between −0.023 and +0.017. The only nominally significant positive entries are **anchor-near AUC gains** (`tg_dec5` +0.003 in split A, p = 0.006; `tg_curv` +0.004 / +0.003, p = 0.012 / 0.028): if one wanted to claim anything it would be an anchor-near effect of ≈ 0.003–0.004 AUC, inside the stratum where test images are within 0.03 of a typed ideal image and the learner is already near-saturated; the same curvature candidate loses 0.012–0.0135 log-loss (p < 1e-4) and about 0.01 HTR accuracy (p ≈ 0.018). The gentle chain (`tg_dec5_mu0.3`) shows far-accuracy gains of +0.008 / +0.017 (p = 0.055 / 0.023, uncorrected, one of 36 secondary cells) — a post-hoc observation that is checked on spare seeds in §6.

*Red-team time-blocked split* (35 contexts, secondary, uncorrected): AUC differences 0.0000 (`tg_dec5`), +0.0002 (`tg_dec5_mu0.3`), −0.0002 (`tg_curv`); accuracy +0.0015 / +0.0029 / +0.0004; none significant; the in-block stratum (both test images inside a held-out block) is likewise null (n of such judgments is small). The development hint (+0.004 AUC for chain ±2 and curvature in the blocked split, p ≈ 0.005 over 20 seeds) did **not** replicate for the frozen candidates. The expectation that "temporal edges should help most there" is not supported.

## 6. Exploratory runs after the freeze (spare seeds 9285–9299, not pre-registered)
Two exploratory runs on the 15 spare seeds 9285–9299 (not pre-registered; per-judgment predictions from `tg_audit_time.py`, learners `lap3_k10_st4`, `aw8_all_st2lr0.5`, `tg_dec5`, `tg_dec5_mu0.3`; one draw per budget; the tables below are `tg_tables.py audit`):

1. **Replication of the post-hoc far-accuracy hint of `tg_dec5_mu0.3`** (confirmation: far accuracy +0.008 / +0.017): on the new seeds it is −0.005 (A) / +0.010 (B), p = 0.34 / 0.44; AUC +0.0009 / +0.0003 overall. **Not replicated** — consistent with noise. `tg_dec5` is again ≈ 0 (AUC −0.0001 / +0.0014).
2. **Is the incumbent's advantage a temporal near-duplicate effect?** Strata by the frame gap between the test judgment's farther image and the nearest labelled frame of the same run (≤ 5 frames: 25 % of judgments; 6–15: 35–40 %; 16–40: 22–27 %; > 40: 13 %). The gain of `lap3_k10_st4` over the tuned graph-free `aw8_all_st2lr0.5` is *not* concentrated in time-adjacent judgments: in split A it is +0.030 / +0.018 / +0.003 / +0.032 accuracy over the four gap strata (the farthest stratum is as large as the nearest); in split B it is ≈ 0 for small gaps and +0.023 (not significant) for gaps > 40. So temporal proximity to a labelled frame is not what drives the learner's gain; the stratification that does matter is the **anchor-near / anchor-far** one: over these 15 seeds the incumbent's AUC gain over the tuned graph-free learner is +0.016 / +0.007 overall but −0.001 / −0.004 in the anchor-far stratum (accuracy +0.026 / −0.009). This agrees with the red-team branch's pre-registered finding (`RESULTS_redteam.md`: the +0.02 AUC gain is carried by judgments within ≈ 0.001 of a typed ideal image; far from anchors it is +0.003 / +0.001 with intervals containing 0); it is a replication-level observation here, not a new result, and the far stratum has only ≈ 30 % of ≈ 60 judgments per cell.

## 7. Limits
- Same 168 pair groups in every seed; development used 20 seeds and 18 learners, so the development winners are selected (winner's curse), and the confirmation has 3 candidates only.
- One random label set per (seed, budget); the paired differences between learners are small relative to the label-set noise, which is why the per-seed Wilcoxon over 35 seeds is the unit of evidence.
- The shuffled-time control is harsh (random within-run pairs are feature-distant); it is a necessary, not a sufficient, check. The local shuffle and random-kNN-edge controls were only run in development.
- Test sets of the A/B splits have 50–70 decisive judgments per seed; the anchor-far stratum has ≈ 30 % of them, so far-stratum numbers are noisy.
- Everything uses frozen SimCLR features; temporal information may matter more for a representation that is not already near-invariant to adjacent-frame differences (GPU list).

## 8. Literature (search snippets only; `notes_temporal_graph_literature.md`)
Temporal-coherence regularisers (Mobahi et al. 2009) and slowness (Wiskott & Sejnowski 2002) motivate first-difference penalties; Hessian-energy regularisation motivates the second-difference variant; none of the found work tests temporal edges inside a semi-supervised preference learner with a few hundred frames, and RHEED analyses that use time mostly segment videos without labels.

**Short answer: no.** None of the four frozen candidates met the pre-registered success rule, in confirmation 1 (§4.1) or in the independent confirmation 2 (§5.1; time-blocked split of confirmation 2 not run for lack of time). Using the latent-type posteriors in the head — as soft pseudo-anchors, as answerability weights, or as an auxiliary target — changes the held-out AUC of `lap3_k10_st4` by about 0.001–0.003 at most (positive for the auxiliary-target candidates, negative for the pseudo-anchors), none of the positive increments is Holm-significant in the standard or anchor-far family (the only Holm-significant results are negative: the pseudo-anchor candidate C4 in split A). The model itself behaves as it should; the head just has little to gain from it.

1. **Does the latent-class model learn the generating process?** Yes, in the sense that matters for the controls. EM on ≤ 90 labelled judgments gives the same table across seeds (§3.1 text and `PROGRESS_LOG`): when both images are of type k the outcome is a tie 59 % (10 judgments) → 68 % (60 judgments) of the time and not_apply only 4–6 %; when exactly one is, the typed image wins 73 % → 81 %; when neither is, not_apply ≈ 60 %. Random memberships (same confidence distribution) are clearly worse than the real posteriors in the head (§4.3: candidate minus random control AUC +0.013…+0.015) and a shuffled prior is worse than the real one (+0.004…+0.006), so the posteriors do carry information.
2. **Does the outcome likelihood add anything over the graph prior alone (the rejected self-training in disguise)?** A little, for one candidate. C1 (`aux0.5`, auxiliary cross-entropy to the posterior) beats its prior-only control in all four standard cells (AUC +0.0014 / +0.0024 in A / B, accuracy +0.004 / +0.001; only B-AUC nominally significant, p = 0.007 uncorrected); in confirmation 2 it is positive in all four cells again but smaller and not significant (AUC +0.0015 / +0.0006, accuracy +0.001 / +0.001, §5.3). C2 does not beat its control in all four cells in either confirmation, C3 beats it only in split B (AUC +0.003, p = 0.007 uncorrected in confirmation 2) (§4.3, §5.3). Where the outcomes help most is relative to bad memberships: random memberships are worse than the real posterior by +0.010…+0.015 AUC in both confirmations. The leave-anchors-out diagnostic says the same: with 25 % of the typed anchors hidden the graph prior recovers their types in 76.3 % of the cases, prior + outcomes in 76.6–77.0 % (§3.1).
3. **Anchor-far stratum (the open gap).** The increments of C1–C3 are positive but small and nowhere Holm-significant (C1 AUC +0.006 / +0.010 in A / B in confirmation 1 and +0.003 / +0.008 in confirmation 2, split B far being the only cell that is positive and nominally significant in both, uncorrected p = 0.008 and 0.005, Holm 0.113 and 0.083, §4.2, §5.2; by distance bin the C1 increment sits at 0.01–0.03 and beyond rather than near anchors, but the intervals mostly include 0, §4.2b). The graph-free control is far behind the baseline in the standard endpoint (AUC −0.019 / −0.017, §4.4), as in earlier studies. Nothing here closes the gap: judgments far from every typed anchor remain essentially at the baseline's level.
4. **Standard endpoint.** In confirmation 2 the standard AUC gains of C1 are −0.002 / +0.002 (A / B), of C2 −0.001 / +0.000, of C3 −0.002 / +0.000: the small positive confirmation-1 values for split A (+0.0003 for C1) did not replicate in sign. Pooled over the 70 seeds (§5.5, secondary): C1 AUC −0.0008 (A) / +0.0024 (B, uncorrected p = 0.016); C3 accuracy B +0.0045 (p = 0.007 uncorrected); C4 −0.0047 (A, p = 0.0002).
5. **Time-blocked split (confirmation 1).** No candidate is better than the baseline; the answerability weighting and the pseudo-anchors are significantly worse (§4.5).
6. **Which uses fail and why.** Soft pseudo-anchors hurt in split A in development and confirmation (they add the graph prior's errors as supervision). The inference-time shift (adding the table's log-odds to the head's logit) hurts for every λ ≥ 0.5 (§3.2, D3b) — the head already contains what the prior says. Answerability weighting is neutral. The graph-wide auxiliary target is neutral.

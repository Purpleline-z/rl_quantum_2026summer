**(b) Exchange rate (estimate with intervals, no significance claim; confirmation 1, 35 fresh seeds, both splits pooled).** Typed labels of ideal images are worth a lot at low pairwise budgets and then saturate quickly.
- *First typed labels.* Going from 0 to 12 anchors (10% of the 116) at 10 extra pairwise judgments raises AUC by +0.017 (accuracy +0.024, −log-loss +0.042); that equals about **23 extra judgments (95% interval 14–35) by AUC, 29 (16–54) by accuracy** — roughly **2 judgments per typed label** (1.2–2.9 by AUC). Starting from 20 extra judgments the equivalent is 23 (12–37) by AUC, from 40 it is 18 (5–38): the same size, but the anchors' gain shrinks as judgments accumulate (AUC gain of 12 anchors: +0.028 at 0 judgments, +0.017 at 10, +0.005 at 40, −0.003 at 60).
- *Next typed labels.* From 12 to 29 anchors the equivalent is 89 (32–285) judgments at 10 extra judgments — **beyond the 60 extra judgments measured**, so it rests on the log-axis extrapolation; read it as "at least tens of judgments" (the lower interval end is 32). The model-free statement: with 29 anchors and 10 extra judgments the AUC (0.920) already exceeds that of no anchors and 60 extra judgments (0.916); 116 anchors with 10 judgments give 0.930.
- *Diminishing returns.* Beyond 29 anchors each doubling adds only +0.003 to +0.007 AUC (+0.004 to +0.012 accuracy); all 116 anchors add +0.050 AUC at 0 judgments and +0.016 at 60 judgments over no anchors. The 144-anchor original (with the 28 outer-test images as anchors) is not better than the 116-anchor version used here (144 − 116: AUC −0.002, accuracy −0.003, both n.s.).
- *Per type.* The benefit goes to c(6x2) (accuracy +0.05 to +0.12 at 0–40 extra judgments with ≥ 29 anchors but only +0.00 to +0.02 at 60, where the anchor-free curve catches up; about 16–31 judgments equivalent for the first 12 anchors) and (1x1) (+0.05 to +0.09; equivalent beyond range), and to RT13 only from 29 anchors on (+0.005 to +0.037). **HTR gains nothing from anchors in accuracy or AUC** (at ≥ 10 extra judgments the accuracy changes lie between −0.005 and +0.004 with intervals covering 0; AUC −0.005 to −0.013 for 12 anchors at ≥ 10 judgments) — its pairwise ranking is already at AUC ≈ 0.99 without anchors; only its log-loss improves (+0.03 to +0.04, an equivalent of about 5 judgments per label), i.e. calibration, not ranking.
- *External endpoint.* Type accuracy of the 28 outer-test ideal images rises 0.73 (12 anchors) → 0.82 (29) → 0.85 (58) → 0.85 (116) and does not depend on the pairwise budget.
- *Caveats that matter for the lab's decision:* this is an exchange rate in **labels**, not minutes; it is for ideal-image anchors (no typed labels of trajectory frames exist to evaluate); it is large at small pairwise budgets and shrinks as judgments accumulate; estimates beyond 60 extra judgments are extrapolations; the per-type numbers rest on few test judgments. What the numbers support: before spending more on pairwise judgments, 12–29 typed anchors are the better first purchase whenever one typed label costs less than about 2 judgments; beyond about 30 anchors (for the types that benefit) more typed labels add little.

**(a) Active anchor rules (pre-registered rule, confirmation 1): not confirmed.** None of the four candidates (`htrq`, `kcf`, `kcg`, `typi`) beats random anchors of the same count on the preference endpoint: 0 of 4 gains positive for `htrq`, `kcf` and `typi`, 1 of 4 for `kcg`, no Holm-significant test (smallest Holm 0.10); the gains are slightly negative (AUC −0.001 to −0.010, accuracy −0.015 to +0.003). The graph rules do not beat their shuffled-graph controls (`htrq` and `kcg` are even a little worse than their controls in split B). Diversity-seeking rules shift the anchor mix away from c(6x2) and (1x1) (for example `kcg`: 5.3 c(6x2) anchors instead of 10.4 for random), the types that profit most from anchors — but this is not a demonstrated cause: in a development check, class-balanced random anchors (a diagnostic that uses the true classes) are no better than random (AUC +0.001/+0.002, accuracy +0.006/0.000) and a class-balanced k-center is not better either (section 4). **Secondary, descriptive:** the same diversity rules improve the *external* type accuracy of the 28 test ideal images (`kcf` +0.039 / +0.044, Holm 0.001 / 0.0005; `typi` +0.026 / +0.035, Holm 0.04 / 0.001; `kcg` +0.024 / +0.022, Holm 0.064) and HTR log-loss (+0.02 to +0.034); the HTR-quota rule lowers the external accuracy (−0.036 / −0.037). So if the goal is the paper's classification endpoint, diverse anchors help; if the goal is pair-preference prediction, random anchors are as good as any rule tried.

**Interaction with the Laplacian term (development, 10 seeds):** the Laplacian regulariser helps at every anchor count (λ = 3 vs λ = 0: +0.023 AUC with no anchors, +0.031 with 29, +0.026 with 116); λ = 10 equals λ = 3. The graph and the anchors are complementary, not substitutes.

## Graph construction and its effect on semi-supervised learning
- **b-matching graphs.** Jebara, Wang, Chang, "Graph Construction and b-Matching for Semi-Supervised Learning", ICML 2009, pp. 441-448 (confirmed metadata). (摘要) b-matching gives every node the same number of edges (a regular graph) unlike greedily built kNN; experiments on artificial and benchmark data report that b-matched graphs are more robust and give significantly better prediction accuracy with LGC, GRF and GTAM, at similar computation time. -> variant: a degree-regular graph (greedy b-matching approximation) vs kNN. https://icml.cc/Conferences/2009/papers/188.pdf
- **Mutual kNN and hubs.** Ozaki et al., "Using the Mutual k-Nearest Neighbor Graphs for Semi-supervised Classification of Natural Language Data", CoNLL 2011. (摘要) Mutual-kNN graphs combined with maximum spanning trees consistently outperformed kNN graphs on word-sense disambiguation and document classification; the authors attribute this to resistance to hub formation. Caveats from the same search: mutual kNN can leave nodes with no edges; a UW report found label propagation slightly worse under mkNN+MST (the best graph depends on the propagation algorithm). https://aclanthology.org/W11-0318.pdf
- **Hubness.** Radovanovic et al., JMLR 11 (2010) "Popular nearest neighbors in high-dimensional data" (confirmed title). (摘要) hubs arise in high-dimensional kNN graphs; in epsilon-neighbourhood graphs (connect if distance below a threshold) hubness does not occur. -> variant: epsilon / adaptive-threshold graph. https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/radovanovic10a/radovanovic10a.pdf
- **Empirical studies of graph construction for SSL.** de Sousa, Rezende, Batista, "Influence of Graph Construction on Semi-supervised Learning", ECML PKDD 2013, LNCS 8190, pp. 160-175 (confirmed metadata; the search showed no results text). A later paper cited in the same search says regular graphs can beat kNN because kNN generates hubs in high dimensions (secondary, not that paper's claim).
- **Graph learning from smooth signals.** Kalofolias, "How to Learn a Graph from Smooth Signals", AISTATS 2016 (PMLR 51, pp. 920-929). (摘要) learns edge weights under a Laplacian smoothness penalty; a secondary site says a log-barrier on degrees prevents isolated nodes (UNVERIFIED). Gaussian-weight construction is a special case. -> variant: Gaussian-weighted edges (a cheap member of this family); a full graph-learning solver was not implemented.
- **Local scaling.** Zelnik-Manor & Perona, "Self-Tuning Spectral Clustering", NIPS 2004 (confirmed). (摘要) per-point scale in the affinity gives better clustering for multi-scale data; the formula exp(-d^2/(sigma_i sigma_j)) with sigma_i = distance to the 7th neighbour is from a third-party page (UNVERIFIED). -> variant: locally scaled Gaussian weights.
- **Shared nearest neighbours.** The search found hubness-aware shared-neighbour distances (Knowledge and Information Systems, supervised kNN classification) but nothing on Jarvis-Patrick graphs for semi-supervised learning: the SNN variant here is motivated by the hubness literature only.

## Why graph regularisation can fail (beyond Nadler et al., already in the other notes)
- El Alaoui, Cheng, Ramdas, Wainwright, Jordan, "Asymptotic behavior of lp-based Laplacian regularization in semi-supervised learning", COLT 2016 (PMLR 49, pp. 879-906; confirmed). (摘要) in a d-dimensional geometric random graph with a fixed number of labels, there is a phase transition at p = d + 1: p = 2 (the usual Laplacian) is degenerate when d >= 2 (estimate dominated by spikes at the labelled points), and p = d + 1 balances smoothness and sensitivity to the unlabelled distribution. Relevance: with 144-154 ideal anchors and a 512-d feature space the plain quadratic penalty is in the degenerate regime in theory; in this study the penalty acts on the head's outputs (a few smooth functions), not on a free node function, so the theorem does not apply directly (my reading).

## Non-graph smoothing controls
- **Interpolation Consistency Training** (Verma et al., IJCAI 2019 / arXiv 1903.03825, confirmed). (摘要) the prediction at an interpolation of unlabeled points should match the interpolation of predictions; moves the boundary to low-density regions. -> control "mixup consistency on unlabelled nodes".
- **Manifold Mixup** (arXiv 1806.05236, "Manifold Mixup: Better Representations by Interpolating Hidden States"; venue/year not confirmed by the search, I believe ICML 2019: UNVERIFIED). -> control "hidden-state mixup consistency".

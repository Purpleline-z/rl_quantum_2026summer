# Literature notes — CPU partial fine-tuning (WebSearch, 2026-10-14; only what the search results state; "(摘要)" = from the search summary, not the paper text)

- **L2-SP** (Li, Grandvalet, Davoine, "Explicit Inductive Bias for Transfer Learning with Convolutional Networks", ICML 2018; arXiv 1802.01483) (摘要): replaces the weight-decay anchor 0 by the pretrained weights, so the penalty only acts when fine-tuning drifts away from the starting point; reported as a standard baseline for transfer learning; extra cost negligible. → our `sp` term (sum of squared layer-4 parameter drift).
- **LP-FT** (Kumar et al., "Fine-Tuning can Distort Pretrained Features and Underperform Out-of-Distribution", ICLR 2022; arXiv 2202.10054) (摘要): with a randomly initialised head, the lower layers change while the head is still being learned and distort the pretrained features; fine-tuning averaged ~2 % better in distribution but ~7 % worse out of distribution than linear probing on 10 shift datasets; linear probing first, then fine-tuning (LP-FT) reduced the distortion. → our stage 1 (head trained on frozen features = the incumbent recipe) followed by stage 2 (joint refinement) is an LP-FT analogue; the anchor-far stratum is our out-of-distribution slice.
- **WiSE-FT** (Wortsman et al., "Robust fine-tuning of zero-shot models", CVPR 2022; arXiv 2109.01903) (摘要): linear interpolation between the original and the fine-tuned weights, theta = (1-a) theta_0 + a theta_1, recovers robustness under shift without extra inference cost; default a = 0.5. → our `wise` option (interpolation of the refined layer-4 weights).
- Mechanistic expectation used for the screens: our unlabelled/test images are near-duplicates of training frames and anchor-far judgments are the only place where a better representation could matter; LP-FT/L2-SP/WiSE-FT all aim at keeping the pretrained geometry, i.e. they predict *small* changes of the far-stratum AUC. A large gain would need genuinely new information (the pair labels) to reach the encoder.

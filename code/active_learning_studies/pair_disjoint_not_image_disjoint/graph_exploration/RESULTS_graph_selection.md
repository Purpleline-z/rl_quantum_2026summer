# Graph selection under the tuned learner (`aw8_all`): do graph rules still beat Random, and can a graph rule help HTR?

Branch `claude/graph-selection-htr-1012`, run of 2026-10-12 (Prompt 2). All tables below are generated by `new_directions/gsel_report.py` from the result JSONs in `new_directions/results/gs_*` (nothing is hand-copied; this file is built from `RESULTS_graph_selection.template.md`). Confirmation numbers are from pre-registered blocks (`PREREGISTRATION_S1.md` + Amendment 1, `PREREGISTRATION_S2.md`, `PREREGISTRATION_S3.md`); development screens (seeds 8000–8019) are labelled as such and are not evidence. The academic report `code/ACADEMIC_REPORT_DRAFT.md` is untouched.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Pre-registered criterion (A) — mixed, and I would not call it a clean success.** The criterion: a graph rule beats Random on accuracy AND AUC, Holm-significant in both splits, at regular or cold start. S1 (35 fresh seeds per block, Holm over 16 tests) missed it narrowly: regular start `qcov_alloc` had three of four cells Holm < 0.05 (B-split AUC 0.062) and `tdc` two of four; cold start `tdc` and `qcov_alloc` had significant AUC cells but no significant accuracy cell in both splits. S2 (30 fresh seeds per block, replication chosen after seeing S1) did not meet it either. **S3 (40 fresh seeds per block, Holm over 8 tests; also chosen after seeing S1/S2) did meet it twice: `qcov_alloc` at regular start (all four cells Holm ≤ 0.033) and `tdc` at cold start (all four cells Holm ≤ 0.014).** Overall that is 2 of 12 rule × block checks, both in S3. Blocks of 30–40 seeds therefore disagree with each other more than the within-block p-values suggest, which is what a small true effect (+0.004 to +0.016) plus split-to-split noise looks like.
2. **Pooled over S1 + S2 + S3 (105 seeds per start mode; descriptive, not a test)** the two typed-coverage rules give +0.005 to +0.012 in accuracy and AUC over Random, bootstrap intervals above zero in all 16 cells. For scale: under the tuned learner the *whole* learning curve of Random from 10 to 60 judgments is worth only about +0.01 to +0.02 AUC (section 4), so there is little headroom; "beats Random" here means a few thousandths, not a step change.
3. **The HTR result (criterion B) is met, but not by a graph effect.** On 35 fresh seeds, HTR accuracy and AUC gains over Random are Holm-significant in both splits for the variance-reduction design `vopt_u@aw`, the Laplacian-prior design `gvopt_lap3@aw` and `qcov_alloc` (other-type accuracy not reduced by more than 0.01 for the latter two; `vopt_u@aw` loses 0.011 in split B and so fails that side condition). Their shuffled-graph controls, a plain stronger ridge prior (`vopt_ridge10@aw`, no graph at all) and a class-priority design without any graph (`htr_vopt_prio@aw`) gain as much or more. So: under the tuned learner, variance-reduction selection improves HTR; the graph is not the ingredient.
4. **What the graph does contribute is finding decisive judgments.** The label-spreading type posterior lets typed coverage pick 65% decisive judgments (Random 50%; the same rule with the posterior shuffled or removed: 49% / 45%). That is a real, graph-specific effect on *what is selected* (section 4). It buys little on the endpoint: an oracle that picks 100% decisive judgments does no better on dev seeds than the realistic rule (section 4).
5. **Graph specificity is real in direction and modest in size.** AUC differences between the graph rule and its shuffled-posterior control are positive in 11 of 12 block × split cells for both `tdc` and `qcov_alloc` (uncorrected p < 0.05 in 4 of 12 each); against the type-only control `tdc` is positive in 12 of 12 (7 of 12 with p < 0.05). In S3 cold both rules beat their shuffled control on AUC in both splits (p ≤ 0.022; the S3 pre-registration's bar for a graph claim); in S3 regular only in split B. `gvopt_lap3@aw` (Laplacian prior) is never better than its shuffled control or a plain ridge prior, so *that* graph rule has no graph contribution.

What this does not show: that graph selection is useless in general (only this dataset, frozen features, the `aw8_all` learner, single-shot selection, 168 pair groups re-split with new seeds), or that the effects are real on new data (all p-values are optimistic, section 7).

## 2. What was run

Protocol unchanged: judgment-unit protocol (one (pair, type) judgment per query), budgets 10/20/40/60 on top of 10 initial groups, splits A and B, held-out decisive-judgment prediction; learner `aw8_all` (anchor weight 8, all 144 ideal images as anchors) for every arm; Random = mean of 5 draws; gain = per-seed mean over budgets of (arm − Random), log-loss sign flipped; seed-level two-sided Wilcoxon; Holm within each pre-registered family. Selection is single-shot from the model fitted on the initial judgments (as `nd_select.py`). Graph = cosine kNN (k = 10) over candidate, revealed and reference images; type posterior = label spreading of the typed reference images; P(decisive) = ridge logistic regression on revealed judgments with 8 graph features + type one-hot.

Candidates (Holm family members): `vopt_u@aw` (non-graph I-optimal design, selection model fitted with `aw8_all`), `gvopt_lap3@aw` (Laplacian-prior design, λ = 3), `tdc` = `typed_decisive_coverage`, `qcov_alloc` (typed coverage with fixed type quota t1x1 .25 / c(6x2) .30 / RT13 .10 / HTR .35). Comparator arms: `tdc_shuffled`, `tdc_typeonly`, `gvopt_lap3_shuffled@aw`, `vopt_ridge10@aw`, `qcov_alloc_shuffled`, `qcov_alloc_typeonly`, `htr_vopt_prio@aw`. ("Shuffled" permutes the type posterior over the nodes (and, for `gvopt`, the adjacency); "type-only" removes the reference/graph features from P(decisive).)

Blocks: S1 confirmation 1 = regular start, seeds 8100–8134; confirmation 2 = cold start (10 random initial judgments), seeds 8200–8234; confirmation 3 = HTR block, seeds 8300–8334; S2 replication = cold 8340–8369 and regular 8370–8399 (30 seeds each); S3 larger replication = regular 8400–8439 and cold 8440–8479 (40 seeds each; `PREREGISTRATION_S3.md`, written after S1/S2, the oracle diagnostic and the sequential dev screen). Each block was run once.

Honest account of the pre-registration chain (all in git history):
- `PREREGISTRATION_S0.md` (dev plan) and `PREREGISTRATION_S1.md` were committed before any seed ≥ 8100 ran. S1 named `htr_cov_amb` (HTR-vs-RT13 ambiguity coverage) as its fourth candidate.
- Amendment 1 (08:25 UTC, before any confirmation seed was analysed) replaced it by `qcov_alloc` after dev screen 5 finished; S1 had been committed while dev 5 was still running, and the confirmation chain started seconds before I read dev 5; the first three result cells of block 1 were deleted unread. This was a deviation from "freeze before you look" and is therefore stated here. `qcov_alloc`'s type quota was chosen by eye on dev seeds, so its dev effect is probably overstated.
- `PREREGISTRATION_S2.md` was written after reading blocks 1–3, so S2 is a replication of hypotheses chosen after seeing S1, not an independent test of a hypothesis fixed in advance.
- The HTR/RT13 ambiguity and bridge rules that the prompt asked for were screened on dev seeds only (section 5) and were not confirmed.

### 4.2 Why the gains are small: little headroom under the tuned learner

### 4.3 Why HTR gains but the others do not

Per-type accuracy gains on the dev seeds (dev1) show RT13 saturated (gain ≈ 0) and the gain concentrated in HTR and c(6x2); that is what motivated the quota `qcov_alloc`. In the confirmation block 3 every rule that does variance reduction on the head's last layer gains in HTR whether or not a graph is involved, which is why the HTR/RT13-ambiguity weighting adds nothing on top (dev, section 5).

## 6. Sequential extension (protocol extension, dev only unless stated below)

`gsel_seq.py` runs the same rules sequentially (rounds of 10, selection model and P(decisive) predictor refitted on all revealed outcomes after every round; the learner is still `aw8_all`; evaluation at 10/20/40/60 revealed judgments). This is a **protocol extension** (the plan fixed single-shot selection) and was only run on the 20 dev seeds, so it is exploratory. Motivation: P(decisive) is fitted on only the 30 initial judgments in the single-shot run, and adaptivity could help it.

Result: sequential adaptation did not produce larger gains than the single-shot runs (dev gains over Random in the generated dev table `gs_dev7_seq`: typed coverage `tdc` AUC about +0.006 in both splits, accuracy +0.008/+0.009; `qcov_alloc` AUC +0.009/+0.010; HTR gains of the variance-reduction designs about +0.045/+0.020 as in single-shot). The decisive share of the `tdc` batches is about the same (68%, versus 67% single-shot on the same dev seeds). With the oracle result of 4.2 this is what a flat headroom predicts. (Per round of 10 the quota of `qcov_alloc` rounds to 3/3/1/3 judgments, so the sequential `qcov_alloc` allocation is .30/.30/.10/.30, not .25/.30/.10/.35.)

## 7. Limitations

- Same 168 pair groups in every seed (only the split changes), so seed-level Wilcoxon p-values are optimistic; a significant result describes this dataset's split variability, not new data.
- Frozen SimCLR features and a small head; an end-to-end fine-tuned model (GPU) may behave differently (`GPU_TASKS_graph_selection.md`).
- The ≤ 4-candidate freeze was kept, but Amendment 1, S2 and S3 are post-looking steps (section 2); the S3 successes are the only ones for the formal criterion and come from a replication whose existence depends on S1/S2 being close but not clearly positive.
- Family choice matters: with 16 tests per block Holm is conservative; the pooled intervals in 3.6 are descriptive and include the S2 seeds chosen after the fact.
- The type quota of `qcov_alloc` was tuned by eye on dev seeds.
- The HTR test set has only about 13–16 decisive judgments per seed; HTR conclusions rest on pooling across seeds and budgets.
- Literature notes (`notes_graph_selection_literature.md`) rely on search-result summaries; no paper text was opened.

## 1. Answers

**Short answer: no.** None of the four frozen candidates met the pre-registered success rule (§4.1 / §5.1). Using the latent-type posteriors in the head — as soft pseudo-anchors, as answerability weights, or as an auxiliary target — changes the held-out AUC of `lap3_k10_st4` by about 0.001–0.003 at most (positive for the auxiliary-target candidates, negative for the pseudo-anchors), none of it Holm-significant in the standard or anchor-far family. The model itself behaves as it should; the head just has little to gain from it.

1. **Does the latent-class model learn the generating process?** Yes, in the sense that matters for the controls. EM on ≤ 90 labelled judgments gives the same table across seeds (§3.1 text and `PROGRESS_LOG`): when both images are of type k the outcome is a tie 59 % (10 judgments) → 68 % (60 judgments) of the time and not_apply only 4–6 %; when exactly one is, the typed image wins 73 % → 81 %; when neither is, not_apply ≈ 60 %. Random memberships (same confidence distribution) are clearly worse than the real posteriors in the head (§4.3: candidate minus random control AUC +0.013…+0.015) and a shuffled prior is worse than the real one (+0.004…+0.006), so the posteriors do carry information.
2. **Does the outcome likelihood add anything over the graph prior alone (the rejected self-training in disguise)?** A little, for one candidate. C1 (`aux0.5`, auxiliary cross-entropy to the posterior) beats its prior-only control in all four standard cells (AUC +0.0014 / +0.0024 in A / B, accuracy +0.004 / +0.001; only B-AUC nominally significant, p = 0.007 uncorrected); C2 and C3 do not beat theirs in all four cells (§4.3). The leave-anchors-out diagnostic says the same: with 25 % of the typed anchors hidden the graph prior recovers their types in 76.3 % of the cases, prior + outcomes in 76.6–77.0 % (§3.1).
3. **Anchor-far stratum (the open gap).** The increments of C1–C3 are positive but small and nowhere Holm-significant (C1 AUC +0.006 / +0.010 in A / B, §4.2; by distance bin the C1 increment sits at 0.01–0.03 and beyond rather than near anchors, but the intervals mostly include 0, §4.2b). The graph-free control is far behind the baseline in the standard endpoint (AUC −0.019 / −0.017, §4.4), as in earlier studies. Nothing here closes the gap: judgments far from every typed anchor remain essentially at the baseline's level.
4. **Time-blocked split.** No candidate is better than the baseline; the answerability weighting and the pseudo-anchors are significantly worse (§4.5).
5. **Which uses fail and why.** Soft pseudo-anchors hurt in split A in development and confirmation (they add the graph prior's errors as supervision). The inference-time shift (adding the table's log-odds to the head's logit) hurts for every λ ≥ 0.5 (§3.2, D3b) — the head already contains what the prior says. Answerability weighting is neutral. The graph-wide auxiliary target is neutral.


## 2. The model and how it differs from the rejected neighbours

Every image carries a latent type z ∈ {(1x1), c(6x2), RT13, HTR, none}. A labelled judgment of the image pair (a, b) for type k has outcome ∈ {a wins, b wins, tie, not_apply}; its probability depends on s_a = 1[z_a = k], s_b = 1[z_b = k] through a confusion table T[s_a, s_b, outcome] (swap-symmetric, shared by the four types, learned by EM inside every fit from the labelled judgments of that fit only; the Dirichlet pseudo-counts only encode the verbal definitions — not_apply: at least one image is not of type k; tie: equally type k; decisive: which is more type k). The prior over z is the label-spreading posterior of the typed ideal images over the cosine kNN graph (α = 0.9; floor 0.05; 'none' mass ρ = 0.15). Judgments of the same image pair for several types form one pairwise factor, so a not_apply for one type and a tie for another type constrain the same two latent types. E-step: loopy belief propagation; M-step: expected counts (unit-tested against brute-force enumeration on a tree). Posteriors are used in three ways inside `lap3_k10_st4` (everything else unchanged; with all terms off the fit equals `lap3_k10_st4` exactly, tested):
- `pa<w>` soft pseudo-anchors: the images of the labelled judgments enter the anchor ranking loss with soft class memberships (weight w × the anchor weight 8) against the hard typed anchors;
- `lik` answerability-aware weighting: a not_apply row pushes down the score of an image with weight P(image is not of the row's type) (instead of pushing both down); a decisive row is weighted by P(at least one of its images is of the row's type);
- `aux<w>` auxiliary target: cross-entropy of the softmax over the four type columns towards the posterior of the judgment images (confidence-weighted); `auxall<w>`: the same on random graph nodes with the posterior fed back through label spreading.
Controls: **`prior`** = no outcome likelihood (the posterior is the graph prior; identical downstream use) — this is the earlier *self-training in disguise*; the difference between `em` and `prior` is exactly the information that comes from the observed outcomes; `random` = EM posteriors permuted among the same images (same confidence distribution, no information); `shufprior` = prior built on the shuffled graph; graph-free `aw8_all_st2lr0.5`.
Difference to the earlier negatives: pseudo-label self-training used the model's own predictions or the graph prior without new evidence (control `prior`); tie / not_apply modelling in the head used them as extra BT terms, here they are evidence about latent memberships; type coupling in the head is replaced by the single latent type per image.

- **The graph prior already encodes answerability.** In all 521 judgments, with the label-spreading prior computed from all typed ideal images, P(not_apply) falls from 61 % (both images with prior mass ≤ 0.1 on the type) to 3 % (one image with mass ≥ 0.9) and P(tie) rises from 6 % to 26 % (`PROGRESS_LOG`, exploration at 02:50; descriptive, uses all data, not an evaluation). The 35 % not_apply judgments are therefore mostly predicted by where the images sit relative to the typed anchors; the outcomes tell the model what the prior already says.
- **The outcome likelihood can only update the few images that occur in labelled judgments** (≈ 38 images at 10 judgments, ≈ 104 at 60), against a graph of ≈ 1100 nodes; only ≈ 6 of 29 hidden anchors lie within 0.01 cosine distance of such an image, so type recovery of hidden anchors moves by < 1 point (§3.1).
- **The head already receives the same information through the Laplacian term and the anchor loss;** adding the posteriors a second time as pseudo-anchors or as an inference-time shift adds the prior's own errors. Where an additional term helped at all (auxiliary cross-entropy), it acts as a weak regulariser towards the posterior, not as new supervision.
- **Far from every anchor** the prior is nearly uninformative and the labelled judgments rarely touch such images; the posterior is then the prior, and the head has no new evidence there. A real gain in that region needs new typed labels or new judgments of far images (`GPU_TASKS_latent_type.md`), not a different use of the existing ones.
- The probability model is deliberately simple (pooled table, single-label latent type plus 'none', conditional independence of the types of one pair given the two latent types); real trajectory frames are mixtures of phases, which a single latent type cannot represent. A per-type table and a mixture state need more judgments per image than the protocol provides.

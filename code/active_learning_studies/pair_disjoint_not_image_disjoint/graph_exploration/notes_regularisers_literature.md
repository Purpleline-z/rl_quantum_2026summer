# Literature notes — regularisers on unlabelled data, graph vs graph-free (study G2)

Only what the search results say (marked "(abstract/snippet)"); nothing here was read in full text (arXiv/CVF are blocked by the proxy; the search tool returns snippets).

- **Manifold regularisation** (Belkin, Niyogi, Sindhwani, JMLR 2006) — a graph Laplacian over labelled and unlabelled points is added as a penalty that punishes different outputs on connected points; it exploits the geometry of the marginal distribution (snippet). This is what `lap3_k10(_st4)` implements for the reward head's scores.
- **Consistency regularisation**, graph-free: Pi-model (two stochastic views of each unlabelled input must give the same prediction), Temporal Ensembling (EMA of predictions as target), **Mean Teacher** (EMA of *weights* as teacher; Tarvainen & Valpola 2017) (snippets). Points useful for the design: noise alone does not help without a consistency target; input augmentation (flips, Gaussian noise) or student dropout is needed; teacher needs no dropout; results are sensitive to the EMA decay (0.99 early, 0.999 later in the paper's recipe); MSE worked better than KL as consistency cost (all from the Mean Teacher paper snippet; benchmarks SVHN/CIFAR-10 with a 13-layer ConvNet, i.e. a very different regime from a 2-layer MLP on frozen SimCLR features).
- **Relation of the two**: Bo et al. (AAAI 2022, "Regularizing GNNs via consistency-diversity graph augmentations") describe label propagation (graph Laplacian) and consistency regularisation as differing mainly in the regularisation term, and use *neighbours as augmentations* with a neighbour-constrained consistency loss, as a supplement to graph regularisation (snippet). That motivates two arms of this study: a consistency loss whose "augmentation" is the nearest neighbour (`rghard*_k1`) versus one whose augmentation is a synthetic perturbation (dropout / noise / mirror / mixup).
- A 2025 JMLR paper (snippet) states that the augmentation-graph Laplacian converges to a weighted Laplace–Beltrami operator on the data manifold as the augmented data set grows: a theory-level link between "many augmentations" and "a graph"; it does not compare the two empirically.
- **No source found** (searched) that compares a pure Laplacian penalty with augmentation-consistency on the same benchmarks; the comparison made here is therefore an empirical one on this data, not a replication.
- Within this repository: `RESULTS_graph_mechanism` (branch `claude/graph-mechanism-20261013`) found that Gaussian input-noise consistency (one or two strengths), weight decay 1e-3/1e-2 and random-pair mixup consistency gave ≈ 0 or negative AUC at the **repository (1×) schedule**; `RESULTS_selection_htr` (sdev screens) found that dropout-consistency `cons0.3_st4` reached most of the gain of `lap3_k10_st4` at the **4× schedule**. The present study is the schedule-matched, pre-registered comparison.
- Why manifold mixup is not run separately: the repository's head has one hidden layer and a linear output layer, so mixing the post-ReLU hidden state with mixed targets from the same head is an identity (checked numerically in the unit tests); mixing the pre-activation equals input mixup.

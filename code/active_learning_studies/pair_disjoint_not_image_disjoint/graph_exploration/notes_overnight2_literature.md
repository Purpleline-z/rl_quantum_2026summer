# Literature reading, overnight run 2 (2026-10-10). Only what search results stated; "(summary)" = search summary, paper text not read (arxiv/ar5iv/CVF are blocked here).

## Graph-regularised optimal experimental design (the family our graph-variance-reduction rules belong to)
- **Laplacian Regularized D-Optimal Design for Active Learning (He, IEEE TIP 2010; summary).** Classical optimal design uses least squares on the measured samples only; the Laplacian-regularised version fits a regression that also preserves the local geometry (a nearest-neighbour graph's Laplacian) and so accounts for unmeasured samples; shown better than conventional designs on image retrieval. -> our `gvopt_lap` puts the graph Laplacian into the prior precision of the head's last layer.
- **G-Optimal Design with Laplacian Regularization (Chen et al., AAAI 2010; summary).** Laplacian-regularised least squares, minimise the maximum predictive variance on the data manifold, sequential selection with Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury updates; better than random, SVMactive and (non-Laplacian) A/G-optimal design on face databases.
- **Manifold Adaptive Experimental Design (Cai and He, IEEE TKDE 2012; summary).** Transductive experimental design in a manifold-adaptive kernel space (graph Laplacian in the kernel); on a two-circles toy TED picked all points from the small circle while the manifold version spread them; text categorisation.
- **Variance minimisation criterion on graphs (Ji and Han, AISTATS 2012; summary).** Gaussian random field over graph vertices; choose a batch (offline) minimising the total variance of the unlabelled vertices' conditional distribution (V-optimality).
- **Sigma-Optimality for Active Learning on Gaussian Random Fields (Ma, Garnett, Schneider, NeurIPS 2013; summary).** Select the node whose labelling most reduces the SUM of all entries of the predictive covariance (variance of the pool-mean prediction), greedy with a (1-1/e) guarantee; reported better than V-optimality for classification, with the intuition that Sigma-optimality queries cluster centres whereas V-optimality goes after peripheral outliers. -> our `gvopt_sigma` / `gvopt_lapsigma`; the pool-wide design `vopt_u` of §5.15 is V-type.
- **Zhu, Lafferty, Ghahramani 2003, Combining active and semi-supervised learning with Gaussian fields and harmonic functions (summary).** Expected-risk minimisation on a Gaussian random field with harmonic mean; high accuracy with far fewer queries than random; costly because the risk is evaluated for every candidate.

## Last-layer Laplace, batch design for deep models
- **Efficient Bayesian Updates for Deep Active Learning via Laplace Approximations (Huseljic et al., ECML PKDD 2025, arXiv 2210.06112; summary).** Last-layer Laplace posterior with a closed-form update after each selected label; sequential batch construction avoids redundant top-b batches. This is the mechanism used by `vopt_u` and `gvopt_*` (rank-one posterior updates inside a greedy batch).
- **A framework and benchmark for deep batch active learning for regression (Holzmueller et al., JMLR 2023; summary).** Describes Bayesian V-optimal design (Cohn 1996) on last-layer features and Bait (Fisher information on last-layer features) as representative of "informative and representative" batch rules.

## Gentle head training = ridge-like regularisation
- **Early stopping for least squares (Ali, Kolter and Tibshirani 2019; arXiv 2406.04425; summaries).** Early-stopped gradient flow is essentially ridge regression with t = 1/lambda; with a fixed learning rate the product (learning rate x number of steps) plays the role of t (our inference); smaller t = stronger regularisation. -> the re-tuned schedule (lr 0.001, 100 steps) and the gentler one (lr 0.0003) act like a strong ridge prior on the head, which is the regime where a design based on the Laplace posterior with ridge = 1 and a graph prior can be consistent with the model; under the old schedule (lr 0.01, 100 steps) the head is far from the prior and the Laplace-based gain vanishes (report 5.15).

## Pair selection under the Bradley-Terry model, and low-label Laplacian regularisation (added later; summaries only)
- **Experimental Design Under the Bradley-Terry Model (Ioannidis, Guo, Dy, IJCAI 2018; summary; the PDF host was not reachable).** Contextual Bradley-Terry = logistic regression on the difference of item feature vectors; pair selection posed as several experimental-design objectives (a D-optimal one on the Fisher information among them), monotone and submodular so greedy gets an approximation guarantee; follow-up (Guo and Dy, SDM 2019) accelerates the greedy search with lazy evaluation. Our `fisher_dopt`, `vopt_u` and `gvopt_*` are in this family (last-layer features of a hidden layer instead of raw features, own-type head).
- **Optimal design for the Bradley-Terry paired-comparison model (Springer, Statistical Methods and Applications; summary).** Static versus local D-optimal designs; local designs depend on assumed utilities (here the current head), which is what the greedy sequential/Laplace versions do.
- **Graph-Laplacian regularisation at very low label rates (Calder and Slepcev's Poisson learning, via a search summary).** Standard Laplacian learning degenerates (nearly constant solution) when labels are scarce; our Laplacian enters as a prior on the head parameters (a penalty on parameter directions along which the score varies across graph edges) and not as label propagation, so this degeneracy does not apply directly, but it is a reminder that Laplacian strength matters at 10-60 labels (we tried lam = 1 and 3 only).
- Searches found no paper that combines a graph-regularised design with a Bradley-Terry last layer for active preference learning, nor graph-aware design for reward models; this is a gap in what we searched, not a verified novelty claim.

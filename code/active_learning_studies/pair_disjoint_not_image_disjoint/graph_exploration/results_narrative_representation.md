# Representation study: does a different frozen representation lift `lap3_k10_st4`?  (branch `claude/representation-20261013`)

All tables below are generated by `rp_report.py` from the result JSONs (`new_directions/results/rp/*`). Pre-registered confirmations and development observations are kept apart: only section 4 (confirmation 1, seeds 8650–8684) is evidence in the pre-registered sense; sections 3 and 6 are development / post-hoc.

## 1. Summary
- **Not confirmed.** None of the four frozen representation candidates improves `lap3_k10_st4` (SimCLR final feature, Laplacian graph, 4x steps) on held-out pair-group AUC and accuracy. Over 35 new seeds and both splits, the 16 pre-registered differences (4 candidates x 2 splits x AUC/accuracy) are **all ≤ +0.0002 AUC and negative in accuracy**; 15 of 16 are negative, and the accuracy losses of the ImageNet-containing candidates (C1, C2) are significant after Holm correction in both splits (−0.017 … −0.024). No candidate reaches the "all four positive" condition, so the shuffled-graph and capacity controls were not run in the pre-registered family (as pre-registered).
- The result of the earlier study **replicates on new seeds**: `lap3_k10_st4` beats the tuned graph-free control `aw8_all_st2lr0.5` by +0.016 AUC and +0.014 … +0.017 accuracy in both splits (Holm p ≤ 0.0002). The representation changes tested here keep part of that gain over the graph-free control in AUC (+0.007 … +0.016) but never add to it.
- **One exploratory signal**: replacing the head input by ImageNet ⊕ SimCLR features while keeping the SimCLR graph (C1, `rp_gs4_his`) improves HTR log-loss by +0.07 … +0.09 (35/35 seeds better, both splits) and HTR accuracy by +0.02 (Holm p ≤ 0.012 in the secondary family), but at the cost of overall accuracy (−0.017/−0.019) and accuracy on √13-type pairs (−0.04 … −0.05). The HTR family is exploratory (≈ 10 held-out decisive judgments of HTR type per split); post-hoc controls on development seeds (section 6) show that the effect needs the graph and is not a capacity effect (it beats the SimCLR ⊕ random-projection control), but it does not make C1 better overall.
- Free variance reduction does not explain anything either: a 5-head ensemble of the baseline adds only about +0.002 AUC and ≈ 0 accuracy (development, one draw, 10 seeds).

Blocks were put on the scale of the baseline feature (norm matching), which keeps the repository's per-budget learning rate meaningful; the schedule was **not** re-tuned per representation.

## 5. Reading of the confirmation
1. **No representation change adds to `lap3_k10_st4`.** The AUC differences are within about ±0.005 of zero or slightly negative (largest loss −0.0089 for ImageNet alone in split B; Holm p = 0.0497); accuracy differences are negative in every cell; C4's AUC loss is also Holm-significant in split B (−0.0064, Holm p = 0.0004). The upper ends of the 95 % bootstrap intervals of the AUC differences are ≤ +0.006 (table in section 4), so an AUC gain larger than ≈ +0.006 from these four candidates is unlikely; this does not exclude gains from encoders or schedules that were not tried.
2. **The graph carries the gain, not the representation.** All four candidates stay well above the graph-free control in AUC (+0.007 … +0.016), i.e. they inherit the Laplacian gain, but C1 uses exactly the baseline graph, and for C2–C4 a plausible (untested) reading is that their neighbourhood structure is similar to that of the SimCLR final feature; swapping the feature does not improve it.
3. **ImageNet features hurt accuracy where the baseline is strong** (C1, C2: −0.017 … −0.024, Holm significant), and mostly on √13-type pairs and c(6x2) pairs, while HTR log-loss improves for C1 and C2 (+0.05 … +0.09) and log-loss improves in split A. The overall picture is a trade-off between pair types, not a uniform gain.
4. **Layer 3 (C3) and the 7-view average (C4)** are close to the baseline (AUC −0.001 … −0.006), consistent with "no information beyond the final feature that the head can use at this label budget". C4 is worse in log-loss (−0.009 … −0.015).

## 6. Post-hoc / exploratory: where does the HTR improvement of C1 come from?
Not part of the pre-registered verdict. Run on development seeds 8600–8619 (not the confirmation block) after seeing the confirmation result, comparing C1 with its shuffled-graph control (`rp_gs4_his_shuf`) and the capacity control (`rp_cap`: SimCLR final ⊕ random 512-d projection of it, same concatenation recipe). Differences are uncorrected paired differences over 20 seeds.

{{TABLE:dev_H}}

Reading (post hoc, development seeds, uncorrected; hypotheses are marked as such):
- On these 20 development seeds C1 reproduces the confirmation pattern against `lap3_k10_st4`: HTR log-loss +0.08 … +0.11 (20/20 seeds better), HTR accuracy +0.015 … +0.021, overall accuracy −0.019, √13-type accuracy −0.05 … −0.06, AUC −0.003 … −0.004.
- **The graph, not the added features, is what keeps C1 competitive**: with a shuffled graph C1 loses 0.15–0.17 AUC (and 0.17–0.20 accuracy) relative to the baseline, i.e. C1 beats its shuffled-graph control by +0.15 … +0.17 AUC (as for `lap3_k10_st4` in the earlier study).
- **C1 is clearly better than its capacity control** (SimCLR ⊕ random projection): +0.010 … +0.012 AUC, +0.015 … +0.028 accuracy, and HTR log-loss +0.07 … +0.09 (uncorrected p ≤ 0.0003 for AUC, accuracy and HTR log-loss in both splits; HTR accuracy +0.05 … +0.08). Simply adding 512 uninformative-but-correlated input dimensions to the head hurts (capacity control minus baseline: −0.013 … −0.016 AUC, −0.034 … −0.046 accuracy), so the ImageNet block adds something that a random projection of the same SimCLR feature does not; its HTR log-loss effect (+0.11/+0.08) is much larger than that of the capacity control (+0.017/+0.005).
- What this does **not** show: that the HTR improvement is useful. It comes with a loss of accuracy on √13-type pairs and overall accuracy, HTR has only ≈ 10 held-out decisive judgments per split, and the HTR family is exploratory. Hypothesis (untested): the ImageNet block makes predictions on rare HTR-type pairs less over-confident (a calibration effect) rather than more accurate; per-type calibration plots would test this.
- Consequence for the pre-registered verdict: none. C1 did not pass the main family (negative AUC and accuracy), so its controls would not have mattered.

## 7. Limits (stated before the run, plus what turned up)
- Same 168 pair groups as all earlier work (new seeds are re-splits, not new data): the p-values are optimistic and the seeds are not independent samples of growth conditions.
- One head schedule and one scale rule for every representation; only a 4-seed, 2-factor learning-rate check was done (stage B2 was stopped early).
- The only second encoder is a cached ImageNet-ResNet-18 feature table (1278 images, no augmentation possible); modern encoders (DINOv2, CLIP, etc.) could not be downloaded (HTTP 403). The conclusion "representation does not help" is therefore restricted to: SimCLR layers 3/4, ImageNet-ResNet-18 final features, deterministic view averages, PCA-whitening.
- The ensemble control used draw 0 and 10 seeds; the stage-B2 re-tuning has 4 seeds per split.
- HTR has ≈ 26 of 144 reference images and ≈ 10 held-out decisive judgments per split; HTR results are noisy and secondary.
- Statistical tests are two-sided Wilcoxon over per-seed mean differences; "not confirmed" is not "no effect" — see the intervals.

## Multi-layer / pre-head features from self-supervised encoders
- SimCLR authors: the representation before the MLP projection head gives better linear-classifier results than the projection output; they conjecture the contrastive objective makes the last layer invariant to features such as colour that can be useful downstream (Google Research SimCLR blog, snippet). Our "final 512-d feature" is already the pre-projection average-pool output, so that point is covered; what remains open is whether *earlier* blocks carry information the last block has made invariant to. (abstract/snippet)
- "Intermediate Layers Matter in Momentum Contrastive Self Supervised Learning", NeurIPS 2021: adding auxiliary losses on intermediate ResNet-50 blocks improves linear probing, mainly medical images (snippet via a secondary summary). Different from using intermediate layers of a model trained without such losses; weak support only. (abstract/snippet)
- I found no search-confirmed layer-by-layer probing study of a *SimCLR ResNet-18*: the choice of layer 2/3 + concatenation is therefore empirical here, not literature-backed.

## Whitening, centring and neighbour structure
- Jégou & Chum, "Negative evidences and co-occurrences in image retrieval: the benefit of PCA and whitening", ECCV 2012: mean subtraction is tied to "negative evidence", axis de-correlation (whitening) to co-occurrences; improves retrieval with short vectors. The snippet reports that whitening can hurt when the output dimension is large because it amplifies noise in low-energy components (snippet, UNVERIFIED in detail). -> variants: whitening with PCA to 64 and 128 dims. (abstract/snippet)
- Suzuki, Hara, Shimbo, Saerens, Fukumizu, "Centering similarity measures to reduce hubs", EMNLP 2013 (confirmed bibliographic data): moving the origin to the data mean suppresses hubs in high-dimensional nearest-neighbour search; follow-up (Hara et al. AAAI 2015) says plain centring works well mainly for small data (< ~500 samples) and proposes localized centring. Our graph already uses mean-centred cosine (nodes ~ 1100), so centring is in the baseline; whitening is the new part. (abstract/snippet)

## Transfer of self-supervised vs ImageNet features
- Ericsson et al., "How well do self-supervised models transfer?", CVPR 2021: the best self-supervised models can match or beat supervised ImageNet representations on most tasks; supervised features held up mainly in few-shot recognition with small domain shift (snippet, natural images). Microscopy / medical snippets (low-data comparative study; dermatology/medical comparisons) point both ways, ImageNet initialisation helps in label-scarce regimes but is colour-rich and domain-shifted. No study of frozen-feature RHEED-like grayscale diffraction images was found. -> no expectation either way for ImageNet features; the concatenation variant tests whether they add anything. (abstract/snippet)

## Ensembling as a variance-reduction baseline
- Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel, Blundell, "Simple and scalable predictive uncertainty estimation using deep ensembles", NeurIPS 2017 (arXiv 1612.01474): M = 5 networks, random initialisation and data shuffling are sufficient to get good performance; a dissertation snippet says deep ensembles consistently outperform bagging. -> control `rp_ens5` (five heads, independent initialisations, scores averaged) is the "free" variance reduction a representation change has to beat; mirror/crop feature averaging is a different kind of averaging (over input views, one head). (abstract/snippet)

## Graph-based SSL on frozen embeddings
- The search found no study of how the quality of frozen self-supervised features affects graph regularisation; scikit-learn documentation and general sources only say that label propagation needs a well-defined, non-noisy graph and that components without labelled nodes receive nothing. So "better features -> better graph -> larger gain" is a hypothesis of this study, not a literature result. (abstract/snippet)

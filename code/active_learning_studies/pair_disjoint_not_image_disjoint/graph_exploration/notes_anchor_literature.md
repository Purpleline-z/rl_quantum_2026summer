# Literature notes for the anchor-exchange study (2026-10-13)
Only what the search results stated; "(summary)" = search summary, paper text not opened. Earlier notes (TypiClust / ProbCover, Sigma-optimality, label spreading, Poisson learning, ...) are in the other `notes_*literature*.md` files and `new_directions/literature/` and are not repeated, except TypiClust, which is used as a rule here.

## Budget allocation between label types / heterogeneous cost
- **"Labels or Preferences? Budget-Constrained Learning with Human Judgments over AI-Generated Outputs" (summary of the Lacuna entry).** Proposes Preference-Calibrated Active Learning (PCAL): the split of the budget between ground-truth labels, preferences and unlabelled points is posed as variance minimisation of the estimator, with acquisition propensities per data type that trade cost against information gain; instance-dependent costs and multi-option preferences are listed as open extensions. The closest match to our question (absolute vs comparative labels under one budget); our version is empirical (an exchange-rate estimate), not an estimator-variance optimum.
- **Settles, "Active Learning with Real Annotation Costs" (NIPS 2008 workshop; summary).** Plain uncertainty sampling can pick informative but slow-to-label items and give no net saving in annotation time; dividing the utility by predicted cost helped when costs are known. Relevance: the exchange rate we estimate is the quantity needed to apply cost-normalised selection across label types once the lab measures its cost per typed label vs per judgment.
- **Cost-effective active learning from diverse labelers (IJCAI 2017) and cost-accuracy-aware adaptive labeling (arXiv 2105.11418; summaries).** Choose instance and labeler jointly by cost-effectiveness; cheap labelers can still be accurate on some instances and the most accurate label is not always the most cost-effective. Relevance: motivates reporting information per label of each kind rather than accuracy alone.

## Joint absolute + comparative supervision
- **Shah and Balakrishnan, "When is it better to compare than to score?" (summary).** Argues that noise from human subjectivity in numeric scores can outweigh their theoretical information advantage, which is the main case for comparisons. Relevance: our absolute labels are categorical type labels of ideal images, not scores, so their reliability is high; this paper does not predict the exchange rate.
- **ROIAL (preference learning with ordinal labels), Huang et al. 2024 (joint regression and pairwise-ranking objectives), CompareXplore (pairwise preference loss + pointwise prediction loss) (summaries).** Training on a comparative and an absolute signal together is used in robotics, regression and hardware design-space exploration, and the combination is reported to help. Relevance: the repository's head is the same kind of joint objective (Bradley-Terry loss + anchor ranking loss); we study the dose of the absolute part.

## Anchor / exemplar selection
- Searches found only general guidance (cluster-then-label: label a few per cluster for diversity plus a few uncertain ones; hybrids of uncertainty and diversity; learning efficiency depends on the initial model) and one few-shot paper that selects the prototypical-network support set actively. No paper on choosing typed reference images for a Bradley-Terry head was found; the anchor rules here (graph k-center, TypiClust, label-spreading ambiguity, HTR-quota) are ours, assembled from the cited ingredients (k-center/coreset, TypiClust typicality, label spreading), not a verified novelty claim.
- **TypiClust (Hacohen et al. 2022; earlier notes).** At very low budgets, pick the most typical point of each of k clusters; used here unchanged as the typicality rule (k = number of anchors; typicality = inverse mean distance to the 20 nearest ideal images).

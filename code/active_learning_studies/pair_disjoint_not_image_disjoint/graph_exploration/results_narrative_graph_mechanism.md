# Why does the graph regulariser help? Graph quality vs. generic smoothing (branch `claude/graph-mechanism-20261013`)

Every table below is generated by `gm_report.py` / `gm_tables.py` from the result JSONs (`new_directions/results/gm/*`); the text around them is hand-written. Same 168 labelled pair groups as all earlier work (re-split with new seeds, **not new data**), so Wilcoxon p-values are optimistic; only the pre-registered confirmation runs on previously unused seeds (8100–8134, 8150–8184) count as evidence; everything on seeds 8000–8019 is development (selected on the same seeds, optimistic). Baselines: `aw8_all` (tuned learner) and `lap3_k10` (the previous branch's Laplacian learner, the new baseline for "better than before").

## 1. Answer in one paragraph
**Pre-registered and confirmed twice (seeds 8100–8134 and, independently, 8150–8184):** (a) *along the noise axis the benefit of the Laplacian regulariser tracks graph quality*: at λ = 10 the per-seed Spearman between the gain over `aw8_all` and the graph's ideal-ideal same-class share is +0.63 … +0.76 for AUC and accuracy in both splits, all four tests Holm p < 1e-4 in each confirmation; degrading the graph with noise removes the gain and turns it negative (AUC ≈ +0.02 at σ ≤ 0.5, −0.01 to −0.02 at σ = 3) and worsens log-loss monotonically. (b) *Switching the penalty off for the RT13 scores* (`gmc_t_no_rt13`: kNN k = 10, λ = 10, RT13 off) beats the previous best learner `lap3_k10` in both confirmations (AUC +0.009 to +0.010, accuracy +0.005 to +0.007, all four differences positive, four Holm-significant tests, beats its shuffled-graph and noise-graph controls and the non-graph control) and beats `aw8_all` by about +0.022 to +0.027 AUC and +0.016 to +0.022 accuracy; log-loss is also better than `lap3_k10` (it is worse for the uniform λ = 10 learner). The uniform λ = 10 learner (`gm_lap10_n0`) is only +0.002 to +0.006 over `lap3_k10` and passed the success rule in confirmation 2 but not in confirmation 1 (1 of 4 Holm-significant), so it is **not robustly confirmed**. **Not shown / negative:** I found no graph construction better than the plain cosine-kNN graph (k = 10) among 49 development variants (mutual kNN, ε-graph, shared-neighbour, Gaussian / locally scaled weights, b-matching, PCA whitening, other encoder layers, ImageNet features, a label-aware LDA topology, skipping near-duplicate neighbours, a self-consistent graph in the head's hidden layer, label-based purification of ideal-ideal edges, λ ∈ {5…40}, k ∈ {5…30}, annealing); several type-"purer" graphs are *worse*, so "ideal-ideal same-class share" is a useful but incomplete proxy (across 26 graphs Spearman ≈ +0.4). Generic non-graph smoothing (input-noise consistency, weight decay, mixup consistency) does not reproduce the gain. The gain is concentrated on the 1x1 and HTR scores; RT13 is slightly hurt by the penalty. The ranking gain is the largest at the smallest budgets and exceeds what `aw8_all` gains from 50 additional random judgments.

## 2. What was added to the protocol
Nothing beyond the previous branch (`PREREGISTRATION_G1.md`): nodes = trajectory + ideal images minus the seed's validation/test pair-group images and outer-test ideal images (SHA-256), head = `aw8_all`, a Laplacian penalty on the head's scores of sampled graph edges. New here: the graph construction, the strength λ, the edges the penalty acts on, the per-type weights, annealing and non-graph smoothing controls are all variable (`nd_graph_mechanism.py`; with the default spec it reproduces `lap<L>_k10` and `lap<L>_k10_n<σ>` to the last digit, unit tests). **Graph quality** = share of same-class edges among edges that join two ideal images with known class (chance ≈ 0.25), measured per seed and split for every graph that is trained on. It is a proxy: it only sees ideal-ideal edges, and for the LDA topology it is circular (the topology used the same labels).

### 3.5 What the development screen showed (observations, optimistic)
- **Noise dose, λ=10:** the AUC gain falls monotonically with noise (≈ +0.02 at σ ≤ 0.5, ≈ 0 at σ = 1–2, ≈ −0.015 at σ = 3), and log-loss gets worse at every step; at λ=3 the dependence is weak.
- **No better graph was found.** Mutual kNN, ε graph, shared nearest neighbours, Gaussian and locally scaled weights, b-matching, PCA whitening, layer-2/3/3+4 features, normalised Laplacian, skipping near-duplicate neighbours, λ ∈ {5, 10, 20, 40}, k ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30}: none is better than `lap3_k10` by more than ≈ +0.003 AUC; PCA whitening (−0.025), the ε graph (−0.018) and Gaussian weights (−0.01) are worse. The plain kNN graph with k=10 sits on a plateau.
- **A graph that is "purer" in type but built in another geometry hurts:** the LDA topology (weight 3, quality 0.99, circular) is −0.03 AUC; the ImageNet-ResNet-18 topology (quality 0.79, close to SimCLR's 0.79) is −0.015 AUC vs `lap3_k10`; concatenating both features gives the same as SimCLR alone. So the ideal-ideal same-class share is not sufficient; what seems to matter is that the edges respect the geometry of the head's own input space (hypothesis; across the 26 graphs Spearman with `overlap_with_head_knn` is +0.43, with quality +0.37, with mean edge cosine +0.40, none strong). A self-consistent graph built in the hidden layer of a first-stage head (round 11) did not help either (3.4d).
- **The dose-response is one-sided.** Degrading the graph hurts, but "purifying" it does not help: cutting the cross-class ideal-ideal edges gives −0.002/−0.001 AUC vs `lap3_k10` (uniform λ=10: −0.005/−0.003 relative to `gm_lap10_n0`) and adding same-class ideal edges hurts (−0.009/−0.004). So type-purity of the ideal-ideal edges is not what the head is missing; the confirmed dose-response says the penalty needs a topology that is not distorted, not that a purer one is better.
- **Generic smoothing does not reproduce the gain:** input-noise consistency (noise the size of a kNN step) +0.002 AUC, weight-decay 1e-3/1e-2 ≈ 0, mixup consistency on random node pairs is harmful (−0.13 to −0.18 AUC, scores explode; a strong "linearity" constraint), annealing the λ is not better than a constant λ.
- **Where it acts:** edges between unlabelled nodes only (5700 edges) or ideal-trajectory edges only (500 edges) each give about a third of the full-graph gain; both together are needed.
- **Which types:** the penalty on HTR scores gives the HTR accuracy gain; on the 1x1 scores the 1x1 gain; c(6x2) ≈ 0; RT13 slightly negative. Switching the penalty off for RT13 (`gm10_type_no_rt13`) is +0.009 AUC over `lap3_k10` in both splits (development), which is the candidate C1.

## 7. How to read this (author's interpretation, hedged)
- **Pre-registered, confirmed on new seeds (both confirmations):** (a) the dose-response — a regulariser that smooths along a degraded graph helps less, and a badly degraded graph hurts; (b) the kNN-graph learner with the penalty off for RT13 is better than the previous best learner `lap3_k10`, and its graph terms matter (shuffled graph −0.07 AUC, noise graph −0.03 AUC, non-graph noise consistency −0.02 AUC relative to it). The effects are small (about +0.01 AUC over `lap3_k10`, +0.025 over `aw8_all`) but consistent across 35 + 35 seeds and both splits.
- **What (a) does and does not say:** "quality" is the same-class share of ideal-ideal edges and the manipulated axis is noise added to the topology features. It supports "a graph that is closer to the data manifold of the head's features gives more gain". It does **not** say that the quality proxy predicts gain across arbitrary constructions: in development, over 26 different λ = 10 graphs the correlation is only about +0.4, and a graph from another encoder (ImageNet: quality as high as SimCLR's) or a label-aware LDA graph (circular quality 0.99) is clearly worse. My hypothesis: what matters is that edges join images that are also close for the head, so the penalty removes variation the head would use anyway, while edges built in a different geometry impose constraints the head's inputs do not support. One direct attempt to exploit it (a kNN graph in the hidden layer of a first-stage head trained on the same labels, alone or united with the input-space graph) gave no gain (−0.006 to +0.004 AUC vs `lap3_k10`), so it stays a hypothesis.
- **"Any smoothing?" — no, for the smoothing I tried.** Gaussian input-noise consistency, weight decay and mixup consistency (nine settings) give about 0 AUC or are harmful, while the graph penalty gives +0.017 to +0.023. Confirmed as a difference to the strongest non-graph control `gm_noise10` (+0.015 to +0.025 AUC). This is a statement about these implementations, not a proof that no non-graph regulariser can work.
- **Where the gain comes from (exploratory, development + confirmation rows):** it is largest at the smallest budgets (10–20 judgments) and has not vanished at 60; unlabelled-unlabelled edges and ideal-trajectory edges each give about a third of the full-graph gain in development (both are needed); it shows up as accuracy gains for 1x1 (≈ +0.03) and HTR (≈ +0.02 to +0.04) and AUC for the other types; the RT13 scores are slightly worse with the penalty (−0.003 to −0.006 accuracy), which is why switching it off helps the AUC; this type-switch adds AUC (+0.004 to +0.006 over the uniform λ = 10 learner) but not accuracy. In "label-equivalent" terms the regularised learners at 10 additional judgments already exceed `aw8_all` at 60 (levels table).
- **Is there a better graph than kNN k = 10?** I did not find one. All constructions that change the topology moderately land between −0.005 and +0.003 AUC relative to `lap3_k10`; the larger ones (PCA-whitened, ε, Gaussian-weighted, ImageNet-only) are worse. So on the frozen SimCLR features the plain kNN graph is on a plateau; the remaining lever is the encoder (GPU tasks).
- **Caveats the numbers do not remove:** (i) the C1 type-switch was chosen on development seeds that share the 168 pair groups with the confirmation seeds; (ii) λ = 10 costs log-loss for the uniform learner; (iii) the HTR accuracy rows are on a handful of held-out pairs per split and are descriptive; (iv) the C2 result flips between confirmations (effect ≈ +0.003 to +0.006), a reminder that effects of that size are at the edge of what 35 seeds on 168 groups can resolve.

## 8. Limits
- Same 168 pair groups in every seed (re-splits), so seed-level independence is only approximate and p-values are optimistic; the type-switch for RT13 (C1) was chosen on development seeds that share these groups with the confirmation seeds.
- Graph quality is almost a monotone function of the noise dose (σ = 0.5 is even slightly above σ = 0), so the dose-response test is in effect a test that the gain falls when the topology is increasingly distorted; it cannot separate "quality" from "amount of distortion" (the across-construction analysis in 3.4 is the attempt to do that, and it is only weakly supportive).
- Graph quality is measured on ideal-ideal edges only (≈ 480 edges, 4 classes); for most trajectory-trajectory edges there is no label. The dose-response axis is noise added to the topology features, which also changes degree and neighbourhood geometry; so the confirmed statement is about this axis, not about "the optimal graph".
- Only one frozen encoder (SimCLR, plus its layers 2/3 and cached ImageNet features for the topology); a better encoder, end-to-end training, learned graphs and prospective data are in `GPU_TASKS_graph_mechanism.md`.
- Non-graph controls: noise consistency, mixup consistency and weight decay were implemented once with one strength each (two for noise/mixup); "no non-graph smoothing helps" means these implementations, not impossibility.
- λ = 10 hurts log-loss (see `ll` columns, exploratory).

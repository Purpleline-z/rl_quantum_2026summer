# Synthetic typed anchors from a kinematic RHEED simulator, and what the anchors' image style does (branch `claude/synthetic-anchors-20261014`)

Every table is generated by `sa_report.py` / `sa_tables.py` / `syn_gap.py` from result files (`new_directions/results/syn/*`); the text is hand-written. Same 168 labelled pair groups as all earlier work (re-split by seed, **not new data**): p-values are optimistic; only the pre-registered confirmations (seeds 11130–11164 and 11165–11199, frozen in `PREREGISTRATION_SA1.md` before any of those cells existed) count as evidence. Development seeds: 11100–11119 (about 45 learners screened on them). Baseline throughout: `lap3_k10_st4`.

## 1. Answer
**Synthetic anchors from a kinematic simulator do not help — confirmed twice. What does help a little is a different thing: the image style of the real typed anchors and a gate that uses a second head trained with style-corrected anchors only where the test pair is far from every anchor. That gate improves held-out AUC robustly, but not accuracy enough to pass the pre-registered success rule.**

**Pre-registered confirmations (35 + 35 independent seeds, splits A/B, each run once, frozen in `PREREGISTRATION_SA1.md`):**
- **C3 `sy_tm_a10` (simulator images as typed anchors, type-aligned, share 0.10): fails in both confirmations.** AUC +0.0024/+0.0018 (conf. 1) and +0.0008/−0.0011 (conf. 2), accuracy −0.0053/−0.0011 (conf. 1) and −0.0065/−0.0048 (conf. 2; split A Holm p = 0.01); no Holm-significant AUC; it is **not distinguishable from its moment-matched Gaussian control** (`sy_tmg_a10`: differences ≤ 0.0005 AUC) and in conf. 2 not from the permuted-type control either (+0.0002/+0.0017 AUC, p ≥ 0.13). So the simulator's images carry no usable information beyond a type-centred cloud of anchors, and the task's success rule (gain over `lap3_k10_st4` **and** over the permuted-type control) is not met.
- **C1 `sy_gate_mask` (anchor-proximity gate): the AUC gain replicates, the success rule is not met.** AUC +0.0042/+0.0037 (conf. 1; Holm p = 0.003/0.002) and +0.0033/+0.0049 (conf. 2; Holm p < 0.001 / < 0.001), all four AUC tests significant after Holm and better than the equal-compute control `sy_gate_base2` (+0.003…+0.005); log-loss better by ≈ 0.008 (p < 1e-4, both confirmations); anchor-far AUC +0.012…+0.023. Accuracy gains are +0.0022/+0.0037 (conf. 1) and −0.0002/+0.0050 (conf. 2): positive in 3 of 4 cells, never Holm-significant (smallest Holm p 0.21), so at most 2 of 4 tests are Holm-significant (rule: ≥ 3, all four positive) in both confirmations → **not confirmed by the pre-registered rule**.
- **C2 `sy_ens_mask` (mean of baseline and masked-anchor head): fails the standard rule, but the pre-registered far-stratum family is confirmed in both runs**: anchor-far AUC +0.021/+0.012 (conf. 1, Holm p = 0.004/0.001) and +0.013/+0.015 (conf. 2, Holm p = 0.02/0.01), far accuracy +0.032/+0.028 and +0.010/+0.027, each better than the equal-compute control `sy_ens_base2` (far AUC +0.012…+0.013, p ≤ 0.005). It pays on the anchor-near stratum (accuracy −0.003…−0.007 per cell; pooled over the 70 seeds, post-hoc: −0.004 / −0.005, p = 0.02 / 0.003) and gains on the standard endpoint only AUC +0.002/+0.003 (conf. 1) and +0.002/+0.002 (conf. 2).
- **Time-blocked split (secondary, uncorrected, 35 contexts each):** the gate's AUC gain is present in both confirmations (+0.0027, p = 0.02; +0.0071, p = 0.0001), accuracy ≈ 0 (−0.0002; +0.0013), log-loss better (+0.0065; +0.0120); the ensemble: AUC −0.0008 / +0.0047, far AUC +0.008 / +0.019; synthetic anchors (C3): AUC +0.0035 / +0.0004, far AUC +0.016 / +0.015 (p ≤ 0.0009) but accuracy −0.003 / −0.002. In the 'both images inside a held-out block' stratum (the part where the 25-frame buffer fully holds) nothing is significant for any candidate.

**Development observations (20 seeds, ≈ 45 learners; not evidence):** a calibrated kinematic simulator gets a synthetic-trained type classifier to 61 % on real ideal images but a real-vs-synthetic classifier still separates perfectly; no synthetic-anchor variant helped; the useful variable turned out to be the **anchor image style** — masking the direct beam in the (1x1) / c(6x2) ideal images and rescaling the 16-bit RT13 / HTR PNGs (clipped by the loader in the project's pipeline: 18 of 154 ideal images are ≈ 88 % saturated) moves the anchors into the trajectory-frame domain, improves the anchor-far stratum by ≈ +0.03 AUC and hurts the anchor-near stratum; the gate/ensemble combine both.

**What this means for the open gap (anchor-far generalisation):** two cheap, independent, confirmed-direction effects exist but are small (anchor-far AUC +0.012…+0.02 from the masked-anchor head; log-loss +0.008 from the gate). The simulator itself contributed nothing; whether a better simulator would is open (see §7). Nothing here tests generalisation to a new growth run.

## 2. Task, and what was (not) available
The question: typed anchors (ideal images) are scarce and valuable (the exchange-rate study: the first ≈ 30 are worth ≈ 2 judgments each); can a simulator supply unlimited typed anchors near every region of the image space, and do synthetic images transfer to the real held-out endpoint? The archive of the existing simulator (`classifier-sto-v6-dual-raster__p656_aligned_peak.zip`, 2250 images of Twinned, c(6x2), root-13) **was not available** in this environment (`find / -name '*p656*'` finds only the validator script), so a kinematic simulator was written from scratch; its 4 types are (1x1), c(6x2), (√13×√13)R33.7° and an **HTR surrogate** (the literature search found no lattice for the lab's "HTR"; the surrogate is 1x1 rods + weak half-order rods + Kikuchi lines + diffuse background and is arbitrary).

**Physical assumptions of the simulator (`rheed_sim.py`).** Kinematic (single) scattering only: reciprocal-lattice rods of the surface lattice and superlattice, exact scattering vector for every detector pixel (Ewald geometry: 15 keV electrons, λ = 0.0994 Å, grazing angle ≈ 2°, detector distance fixed by the measured 65 px rod spacing), Gaussian rod profiles (lateral width = domain size, along-beam width = Ewald thickness / rod thickness), random surrogate rod weights (no structure factors), rotational / mirror domain mixtures with random weights for c(6x2) and RT13, phenomenological Kikuchi lines, diffuse background, shadow edge, specular spot, blur, Poisson + read noise, exposure jitter. No multiple scattering, no dynamical effects, no thermal diffuse scattering, no real surface morphology. Unsure about: the beam energy and azimuth of the real experiment (assumed 15 keV, [100]), the lateral coherence length, which fractional rods the real c(6x2) shows at which intensity. Unit tests check the geometry (rod spacing, Laue circle through the specular spot, lattice areas, determinism).

## 4. A finding about the real typed anchors (independent of the simulator)
- **18 of the 154 ideal images (10 HTR, 8 RT13) are 16-bit PNGs (780 × 580, a different camera with a circular viewport).** The loader's `Image.open(p).convert("L")` clips the 16-bit values at 255: ≈ 88 % of the pixels are saturated and their features are almost constant (≈ 0.37 cosine distance from every trajectory frame). They are 34 % of the HTR anchors.
- **The (1x1) and c(6x2) ideal images are uncropped frames** (direct beam + halo visible above the shadow edge); trajectory frames are not. The direct beam dominates the SimCLR feature: the median distance of a (1x1) ideal image to the nearest trajectory frame is 0.69 (c(6x2): 0.31); after masking the rows above the shadow edge it is 0.17 (c(6x2) 0.22–0.33). Only 20 % of the (1x1) and 5 % of the c(6x2) ideal images are near-copies of trajectory frames — the "anchor-near" stratum of the red-team audit is mostly RT13 / HTR and copy-type anchors.

### 5.7 What development says (hypotheses, not findings)
- Real typed anchors alone give held-out AUC 0.92–0.94 without any pair label; synthetic anchors alone give 0.58–0.60 — above the permuted control (0.41–0.45), i.e. *some* type information crosses the gap, but far less than real anchors; mixing synthetic anchors into real ones lowers the AUC and the accuracy of the (c(6x2), (1x1)) types.
- Shifting the synthetic type means to the real ones restores most of the real-anchor performance (AUC 0.91) and gives a **better anchor-far AUC than real anchors** (+0.02), **but a Gaussian cloud with the same per-type mean and covariance does the same** and the permuted-type version is clearly worse: what helps is the *type-centred spread of the anchor cloud*, not the simulator's images.
- Direct-beam-masked real anchors help the anchor-far stratum (AUC +0.03) and hurt the anchor-near stratum, also with zero pair labels; I do not have a verified mechanism (connectivity hypothesis refuted, §5.1).

## 6. Pre-registered confirmations (frozen in `PREREGISTRATION_SA1.md`)
Two independent confirmations of one frozen specification (3 candidates, 7 controls, baseline `lap3_k10_st4`). **No candidate meets the pre-registered success rule in either confirmation.** The rule is: AUC and accuracy gains positive in both splits, ≥ 3 of the 4 Holm-adjusted tests (Holm over 12 = 3 candidates × 2 splits × {AUC, accuracy}) significant, and better than the candidate's control. The verdict tables below separate the three requirements. Summary of what replicates (same sign and significance in both confirmations): the gate's AUC gain and log-loss gain; the ensemble's and the gate's anchor-far gains over the equal-compute controls; the failure of synthetic anchors. What does not replicate: the gate's accuracy gain (+0.0022/+0.0037, then −0.0002/+0.0050; only split B of confirmation 2 is nominally significant, Holm 0.21).

Pooled over the 70 seeds (**post-hoc, descriptive, not a pre-registered test**): gate AUC +0.0037/+0.0043, accuracy +0.0010/+0.0044 (p = 0.44/0.003 uncorrected), log-loss +0.008, far AUC +0.014/+0.017, far accuracy +0.003/+0.023; the equal-compute control `sy_gate_base2` is ≈ 0 on all of these (AUC +0.0007/+0.0004). The accuracy of the gate is therefore split-dependent (B yes, A no), as the confirmations show.

## 7. What the simulator would need (gap analysis)
The gap analysis says what a useful simulator would have to achieve, with the evidence of this study:
1. **Realism in the encoder's feature space, not in human eyes.** After calibration the simulator reaches the right regions of the SimCLR space (median synthetic → nearest real reference distance 0.04, a synthetic-trained type classifier 61 % on real ideal images for four types, 33/41 and 40/42 for (1x1) / c(6x2)) but a real-vs-synthetic classifier still separates the two perfectly. Feature-statistics alignment (CORAL) brings the discriminator to AUC 0.75 but destroys the type information (type transfer 0.34); only an alignment that uses the real type means restores it (0.86), and then a Gaussian cloud with the same moments is as good as the simulator's images. A simulator that is *useful beyond a type-centred cloud* has to put its images where real, *unlabelled* frames lie (recall: real frame → nearest synthetic image, now 0.06 vs the real nearest-neighbour distance 0.0001): the real data are near-duplicate frames of three runs, and the learners profit from anchors that are near-duplicates of test frames, not from coverage of the space.
2. **Types that transfer.** (1x1) and c(6x2) transfer to some degree; RT13 and HTR do not (7/30, 1/19). RT13 needs real rod weights (structure factors) and the higher-Laue-zone arcs at the observed intensities; HTR needs a physical model from the physics team (lattice, expected streaks) — the surrogate here is arbitrary.
3. **A test that can see more than the frozen SimCLR features:** the discriminator is perfect, so encoder-side adaptation (fine-tuning with a domain loss, style transfer of the synthetic images) is the lever, not more rods (GPU list).
4. **The cheaper lever found here:** anchors in the trajectory-frame style (direct beam masked, 16-bit files rescaled). It acts through the head (zero-pair-label diagnostic) and gives anchor-far AUC +0.012…+0.03 without any simulator.

## 8. Limits
- Same 168 pair groups and three runs; no new run is tested (the central open gap — generalisation to a new growth run — is **not** addressed by any CPU experiment here).
- The anchor-far stratum (≈ 15–30 % of ≈ 60 judgments per cell) is noisy; the gate's threshold equals the stratum definition; the near stratum of the gate is identical to the baseline by construction.
- Development screened ≈ 45 learners on 20 seeds (winner's curse); one draw per cell; calibration used real unlabelled frames and ideal images only (no labelled pair-group image, so no held-out image of any seed).
- The simulator is kinematic, rod weights are random, HTR is a surrogate, and the test of transfer is through frozen SimCLR features only (a different representation, or end-to-end training, might see the synthetic images differently — GPU list).

## 9. Literature (search snippets only; `notes_synthetic_anchors_literature.md`)
Kinematic RHEED geometry is standard (rods × Ewald sphere, broadening by domain size / beam divergence); no source tested synthetic RHEED images as anchors of a preference learner; the general sim-to-real literature (domain randomisation, real-vs-synthetic discriminators) motivates the gap diagnostics; "HTR" is not defined in any source found.

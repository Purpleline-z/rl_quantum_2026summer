## 2. Task, and what was (not) available
The question: typed anchors (ideal images) are scarce and valuable (the exchange-rate study: the first ≈ 30 are worth ≈ 2 judgments each); can a simulator supply unlimited typed anchors near every region of the image space, and do synthetic images transfer to the real held-out endpoint? The archive of the existing simulator (`classifier-sto-v6-dual-raster__p656_aligned_peak.zip`, 2250 images of Twinned, c(6x2), root-13) **was not available** in this environment (`find / -name '*p656*'` finds only the validator script), so a kinematic simulator was written from scratch; its 4 types are (1x1), c(6x2), (√13×√13)R33.7° and an **HTR surrogate** (the literature search found no lattice for the lab's "HTR"; the surrogate is 1x1 rods + weak half-order rods + Kikuchi lines + diffuse background and is arbitrary).

**Physical assumptions of the simulator (`rheed_sim.py`).** Kinematic (single) scattering only: reciprocal-lattice rods of the surface lattice and superlattice, exact scattering vector for every detector pixel (Ewald geometry: 15 keV electrons, λ = 0.0994 Å, grazing angle ≈ 2°, detector distance fixed by the measured 65 px rod spacing), Gaussian rod profiles (lateral width = domain size, along-beam width = Ewald thickness / rod thickness), random surrogate rod weights (no structure factors), rotational / mirror domain mixtures with random weights for c(6x2) and RT13, phenomenological Kikuchi lines, diffuse background, shadow edge, specular spot, blur, Poisson + read noise, exposure jitter. No multiple scattering, no dynamical effects, no thermal diffuse scattering, no real surface morphology. Unsure about: the beam energy and azimuth of the real experiment (assumed 15 keV, [100]), the lateral coherence length, which fractional rods the real c(6x2) shows at which intensity. Unit tests check the geometry (rod spacing, Laue circle through the specular spot, lattice areas, determinism).

**Reading.** After calibration the median synthetic image is 0.04 (cosine distance, centred features) from its nearest real reference (before calibration 0.07); a classifier trained on synthetic features labels real ideal images of (1x1) and c(6x2) well (33/41 and 40/42), but it fails for RT13 (7/30) and HTR (1/19) — the HTR failure is expected (surrogate) — and a real-vs-synthetic classifier still separates them perfectly (AUC 1.000) before feature alignment and 0.75 after CORAL alignment. The synthetic frames cover the real unlabelled frames only coarsely (recall distance 0.06, real frames' own nearest-neighbour distance 0.0001): the simulator reaches the right *regions* of the SimCLR space, not the near-duplicate density of the data.

## 4. A finding about the real typed anchors (independent of the simulator)
- **18 of the 154 ideal images (10 HTR, 8 RT13) are 16-bit PNGs (780 × 580, a different camera with a circular viewport).** The loader's `Image.open(p).convert("L")` clips the 16-bit values at 255: ≈ 88 % of the pixels are saturated and their features are almost constant (≈ 0.37 cosine distance from every trajectory frame). They are 34 % of the HTR anchors.
- **The (1x1) and c(6x2) ideal images are uncropped frames** (direct beam + halo visible above the shadow edge); trajectory frames are not. The direct beam dominates the SimCLR feature: the median distance of a (1x1) ideal image to the nearest trajectory frame is 0.69 (c(6x2): 0.31); after masking the rows above the shadow edge it is 0.17 (c(6x2) 0.22–0.33). Only 20 % of the (1x1) and 5 % of the c(6x2) ideal images are near-copies of trajectory frames — the "anchor-near" stratum of the red-team audit is mostly RT13 / HTR and copy-type anchors.

### 5.7 What development says (hypotheses, not findings)
- Real typed anchors alone give held-out AUC 0.92–0.94 without any pair label; synthetic anchors alone give 0.58–0.60 — above the permuted control (0.41–0.45), i.e. *some* type information crosses the gap, but far less than real anchors; mixing synthetic anchors into real ones lowers the AUC and the accuracy of the (c(6x2), (1x1)) types.
- Shifting the synthetic type means to the real ones restores most of the real-anchor performance (AUC 0.91) and gives a **better anchor-far AUC than real anchors** (+0.02), **but a Gaussian cloud with the same per-type mean and covariance does the same** and the permuted-type version is clearly worse: what helps is the *type-centred spread of the anchor cloud*, not the simulator's images.
- Direct-beam-masked real anchors help the anchor-far stratum (AUC +0.03) and hurt the anchor-near stratum, also with zero pair labels; I do not have a verified mechanism (connectivity hypothesis refuted, §5.1).

## 8. Limits
- Same 168 pair groups and three runs; no new run is tested (the central open gap — generalisation to a new growth run — is **not** addressed by any CPU experiment here).
- The anchor-far stratum (≈ 15–30 % of ≈ 60 judgments per cell) is noisy; the gate's threshold equals the stratum definition; the near stratum of the gate is identical to the baseline by construction.
- Development screened ≈ 45 learners on 20 seeds (winner's curse); one draw per cell; calibration used real unlabelled frames and ideal images only (no labelled pair-group image, so no held-out image of any seed).
- The simulator is kinematic, rod weights are random, HTR is a surrogate, and the test of transfer is through frozen SimCLR features only (a different representation, or end-to-end training, might see the synthetic images differently — GPU list).

## 9. Literature (search snippets only; `notes_synthetic_anchors_literature.md`)
Kinematic RHEED geometry is standard (rods × Ewald sphere, broadening by domain size / beam divergence); no source tested synthetic RHEED images as anchors of a preference learner; the general sim-to-real literature (domain randomisation, real-vs-synthetic discriminators) motivates the gap diagnostics; "HTR" is not defined in any source found.

# Why are HTR judgments "hard"?  Error analysis of `lap3_k10_st4` and a targeted fix

Branch `claude/htr-failures-analysis-6iw4qu`.  Seeds: development 10400-10419 (used), confirmation 1 = 10430-10464 and confirmation 2 = 10465-10499 (**not used**, see §5).  Pre-registration: `PREREGISTRATION_HF.md` (committed before any Stage-2 cell).
Code: `hf_table.py`, `hf_labels.py`, `hf_testmap.py`, `hf_errors.py`, `hf_sheets.py`, `hf_learners.py`, `hf_a1.py` (wrapper of `rt_a1.py`), `hf_blend.py`, `hf_gain.py`, `hf_select.py`, `hf_figure.py`; tests `test_htr_failures.py` (6 pass).
Generated detail: `results/hf/errors_dev.md` (all tables of §2), `results/hf/labels_report.md` (§3), `results/hf/dev_selection.md`, contact sheets `results/hf/sheets*/`, summary figure `results/hf/hf_summary.png`.  Stored predictions: `results/hf/a1/` (rt_a1 format, 20 seeds x A/B x budgets 10/20/40/60 x 3 draws, `lap3_k10_st4` and `aw8_all_st2lr0.5`).

## 0. Headline
1. **On the repository's own endpoint HTR is not a hard type.**  `lap3_k10_st4` gets HTR accuracy 0.956 (Split A) / 0.951 (B) and log-loss 0.162 / 0.166 on held-out decisive judgments (dev, 20 seeds), against 0.69-0.71 for (1x1), 0.82-0.83 for c(6x2) and 0.97 for RT13.
2. **All of the remaining error is two judgments out of 45.**  There are only 45 decisive HTR judgments in the data (each judged once).  Judgment 62 is wrong in 100 % of the 108 test rows it appears in, judgment 101 in 56 % (180 rows); every other judgment has error below 5 % (322: 4.8 %, 42 judgments at 0 %).  Excluding 62 and 101, HTR accuracy is 0.999 in both splits and HTR log-loss falls from 0.16 to 0.09-0.11.
3. **The HTR labels are consistent, so this is not a label-noise ceiling.**  The nearest other comparison (oriented, same type) carries the same winner for 44 of 45 judgments; the label-free rule "the frame nearer to the typed HTR ideal images wins" is right for 44 of 45; the 31 pairs judged decisively for both HTR and RT13 have opposite winners in 31 of 31.  The ceiling suggested by label consistency is about 0.98 (one judgment of 45 conflicts with its neighbours), the learner is at 0.95.  The remaining headroom is 2.4 points of accuracy if judgment 62 (the one conflicting label) is counted as label noise and fixing only 101, and 4.7 points if both are model errors; either way it is two specific comparisons in which neither frame is a clear HTR frame, not diffuse noise.
4. **The targeted fix did not survive the strict test, so nothing was confirmed.**  Using anchor similarity inside the HTR output raises dev HTR accuracy by +0.024 to +0.036 and log-loss by +0.07 to +0.10 on the standard endpoint (several variants Holm-significant in both splits on the development seeds), but with ideal images that are near-copies of held-out frames removed (anchor-strict, delta = 0.01) the same variants lose 0.07-0.29 HTR accuracy.  By the pre-registered selection rule **no variant is eligible, so no candidate was confirmed and the pre-registered success criterion is NOT met** (it was never tested on confirmation seeds).

## 1. Data facts that limit every claim
* 521 usable judgments, 168 pair groups, 284 images.  HTR: 129 judgments = 45 decisive (26 "image 1", 19 "image 2") + 83 not_apply + 1 tie.  No (pair, type) was judged twice and no pair by two labellers, so **repeat-agreement (inter-rater) ceilings cannot be computed**; the 45 decisive HTR judgments use 83 distinct images in 90 slots (a near-matching; no image both wins and loses), so transitivity checks have almost no power (0 cycles at any merge radius, but also none possible).
* A seed re-draws the split, not the labels: each HTR judgment is a test item in roughly a quarter of the (seed, split) cells.  The "~10 HTR judgments per split" are 8-10 of these same 45.  Everything pooled over seeds measures behaviour on these 45 judgments under re-drawn splits and label sets.
* Labellers: AJ 27, HL 13, SY 5 of the 45 decisive HTR judgments; confidence field empty for 30, "Confident" 14, "Somewhat sure" 1.  Only 7 of the 45 are same-run pairs; 38 are HTR-like vs non-HTR-like frames from different runs.
* Ideal images are (near-)copies of trajectory frames: for 40 of the 45 judgments the winning frame lies within 1 - cos <= 0.07 of an ideal HTR image (23 within 0.01).  This is what makes the HTR rule and the earlier anchor effects work (red-team audit) and matters for §4.

Where the errors are (pooled over cells; accuracy of rows in the stratum):
* **Comparison type** (nearest ideal class of the two frames): HTR-vs-RT13 frames 25 judgments, accuracy **1.000** (2664 rows, log-loss 0.046); HTR-vs-1x1 13 judgments, 0.997; the errors sit in the 3 judgments of "1x1-like vs RT13-like" (0.667; one of them is 101), 1 judgment of "c6x2-like vs 1x1-like" (0.000; 62) and the 1x1-1x1 / RT13-RT13 / HTR-HTR singletons (all correct but with log-loss 0.30-0.50).  So the **RT13-HTR boundary is not where the learner fails**; it fails on comparisons between two frames that are both far from HTR, where "which is more HTR" is a graded call that the binary HTR-vs-rest anchor loss does not define and only a handful of pairwise labels (about 6 of 45 judgments are of this kind) determine.
* **Log-loss** is concentrated the same way: judgments 62 and 101 carry 26 % and 17 % of the HTR log-loss, the five worst 62 %.
* **Confidence / labeller**: no usable signal (Confident 0.938, none 0.961; AJ 0.957, HL 0.933, SY 1.000); the difference is carried by judgment 101 (HL, Confident).
* **Temperature / frame position**: failures are at 620-860 C frames (accuracy 0.86-0.90 in the 600-900 C bins, 0.999 above 900 C) because 62 and 101 live there; there is no frame-index effect (same-run pairs 0.993).
* **Anchor proximity**: strata far from every ideal image (nearest-ideal distance > 0.03) 0.89; the near strata 0.94-1.00 (non-monotone: judgment 62's winner is a near-copy of an RT13 ideal).
* **Clusters of failures**: two singletons (62, 101) and one marginal (322); with two failing judgments there is nothing to cluster, and their image pairs are unrelated (different runs and temperatures).
* Contact sheets (`results/hf/sheets/sheet_00.png`, `sheet_01.png`; correct reference cases `sheets_correct/`): **62** pairs a dim, almost featureless 165 C 1x1-like frame with a bright 620 C frame that is a near-copy of an RT13 ideal image; the labeller (AJ, the only judgment recorded for that pair) calls the RT13-like frame "more HTR", the head (trained to push RT13 anchors down in the HTR column) says the opposite.  **101** pairs a 443 C RT13-like frame with a dim 857 C 1x1-like frame; the 1x1-like frame wins (HL, Confident); the same pair's RT13 and 1x1 judgments say frame 1 is more RT13 and more 1x1.  The other "1x1-like beats RT13-like" HTR judgments (209, 226, 176) are learned correctly, so the learner sits on the boundary of this regime and flips on 101.  Typical correct cases are an unmistakable HTR frame (bright central spot with chevron / X streaks) against an RT13 or 1x1 frame.

## 3. Label consistency and the ceiling (`results/hf/labels_report.md`)
* Leave-one-out **1-NN label predictor** (winner of the nearest other same-type comparison, oriented; uses neither images' identity-disjointness nor any model): HTR **0.978 (44/45)**, RT13 0.968, c6x2 0.706, 1x1 0.703.  For HTR, all 64 pairs of comparisons closer than 0.1 agree (100 %); agreement declines only for distant comparisons (0.655 at distance > 0.3).  The single disagreement is judgment 62 against judgment 384 at distance 0.145, not a near-duplicate.
* Rule **"the frame nearer to the ideal HTR images wins"** (cosine on centred SimCLR features, no pairwise labels used): 44/45 (0.978) on all HTR judgments; with ideal images within 0.003-0.01 of the frame removed 0.978 / 1.000 (margin version); with 0.03 removed 0.93, with 0.1 removed 0.89 (the margin version 1.00 / 0.87).  Other simple rules: later frame wins (same-run pairs, n = 7) 7/7; higher temperature wins 0.644; farther from the ideal RT13 images wins 0.80.  Caveat: I tried 7 rules on these 45 labels (all splits share them), so the 0.978 is descriptive, not a validated estimate; the 1-NN agreement is the cleaner number.
* **Cross-type**: where HTR and RT13 are both decisive for a pair the winners are opposite in 31/31 pairs (1x1: 17/22 opposite, c6x2: 9/13).
* **Ties / not_apply neighbours**: 7 of 45 decisive judgments share an image with an HTR not_apply judgment; neither 62 nor 101 does; each of them has all other types of its pair consistent with its label (62 has no other judgment for its pair).
* **Transitivity**: no 2- or 3-cycle at merge radius 0 - 0.03; no power (matching).
* **Reading**: the label-consistency ceiling for HTR accuracy is about 0.98 (one judgment of 45 is in conflict with its neighbours), the learner is at 0.95; HTR is therefore close to, but not at, its consistency ceiling, and the gap is two judgments (2.4-4.7 points).  It is not a noise ceiling in the sense of random label flips: consistent labels leave nothing to explain by noise except possibly judgment 62.

## 4. Stage 2: targeted variants (≤ 8, fixed in the pre-registration)
Diagnosis -> variants: HTR-ness of non-HTR frames is well described by similarity to the typed HTR ideal images, so inject it into the HTR output only: post-hoc blend `d + g (r(a) - r(b))` (g = 5 / 10 / 20; top-1) and top-3 mean (`hfb3_g10`), anchor-similarity features in the head (`hfs`), an anchor-similarity teacher loss on the HTR output (`hfq1`), cross-type coupling `d_HTR - d_RT13` (`hfc1`, `hfcb10` = + blend).  Not built because the diagnosis does not support them: hard-negative anchors / RT13-boundary margin head (HTR-vs-RT13 already 100 %), re-weighting conflicting labels (one conflicting pair), per-type graphs.

* On the **standard** endpoint the blends fix judgments 62 and 101 (dev HTR accuracy 0.956 / 0.948 -> 0.984 / 0.983 for `hfb_g10`) and cut HTR log-loss by 0.07-0.10, significant in both splits for `hfb_g10`, `hfb_g20`, `hfb3_g10` (Holm < 0.05 in all four HTR tests on the development seeds).  `hfq1` also gains on the standard endpoint (HTR accuracy +0.045 / +0.064, accuracy 1.000) and `hfs` gains but hurts c6x2 by 0.04-0.06.  Cross-type coupling does nothing: the RT13 head adds no information about the two failing comparisons.
* Under **anchor-strict** the blends and `hfq1` lose HTR accuracy (-0.07 to -0.29 in Split A) and often log-loss, while the baseline itself barely moves (HTR accuracy 0.942 / 0.944 strict vs 0.956 / 0.948 standard, Split A / B).  The anchor-similarity score is only reliable when a near-copy HTR ideal image exists for the frame; without it, its large logit scale (g = 10-20) overrides a better head.  So these variants **use the dataset property that ideal images are near-copies of the test frames**; they do not learn a better HTR concept.
* **Selection rule** (pre-registered): eligible = HTR accuracy gain >= 0 in all four conditions, other types >= -0.01, AUC >= -0.002.  **No variant is eligible** (the best minimum log-loss gain is `hfs` +0.026, which fails the other-type and AUC constraints; every blend / `hfq1` fails on strict accuracy).  Therefore no candidate was frozen and the confirmation seeds were not used.
* Disclosure: before fixing the variants I looked at a post-hoc blend with g in {2, 5, 10, 20} on the first 12 dev seeds (g = 2 weaker, dropped); the blends were then evaluated on all 20 dev seeds, which therefore are not independent of that preview.  Counting the 3 g-values the variants number 8.

## 5. Verdict against the pre-registered success criterion
* "HTR accuracy and log-loss gains over `lap3_k10_st4` Holm-significant in both splits (pooled HTR judgments, bootstrap by seed) with other types losing <= 0.01 accuracy and AUC not worse by > 0.002": **not demonstrated.**  On development seeds the standard-endpoint criterion is met by `hfb_g10` / `hfb_g20` / `hfb3_g10` (other types unchanged by construction; AUC +0.005), but these fail the pre-registered anchor-strict eligibility, so they were not confirmed; no confirmation cell exists.  This is the pre-registered outcome "reported as depends on near-copy anchors", not an improvement of the learner.
* "If HTR is already near the noise ceiling, that itself is the headline": **the closest accurate statement is §0 items 1-3** - HTR accuracy 0.95 with a label-consistency ceiling of about 0.98, the gap being two comparisons; it is not a noise ceiling, because the labels are consistent.  The lab's impression that learner improvements move HTR "only a few points" is what that headroom predicts (at most 2.4-4.7 points, all in two judgments).  HTR *log-loss* has more headroom than accuracy (0.16 -> about 0.10 if the two comparisons were fit), the same two judgments.
* Seeds 10430-10499 are unspent.  If the lab wants the standard-endpoint blend confirmed anyway (as a statement about "with typed near-copy anchors available"), that would be an explicitly **post-hoc, non-pre-registered** confirmation (candidate `hfb3_g10`, best standard-endpoint dev gain); I did not run it because the pre-registered rule forbids it and its outcome is predictable from the development table.

## 6. Caveats
* Everything uses the same 168 groups; 45 HTR judgments, 2 of them decide the outcome.  Bootstrap-by-seed intervals reflect split / label-set resampling on those judgments, not new data; a different handful of HTR labels could change the picture.  The effect of "judgments 62 and 101" is a statement about these two comparisons (one labeller each), not about a class of images.
* The rule / 1-NN agreement numbers are descriptive; 7 simple rules were tried on all 45 labels.
* The anchor-strict endpoint is the red-team's stricter protocol (removes ~38 % of HTR anchors); it is harsher on similarity-based variants than on the baseline and removes some anchors that are not near-duplicates of the *particular* test judgment.  A per-judgment strictness (only the anchors near that judgment's two frames) would be gentler; the static rule keeps 0.978 under it.
* Real remaining ideas, untested: ordering of non-HTR frames needs more graded HTR labels (e.g. query the lab for HTR judgments between 1x1-like and RT13-like frames, the regime that fails); a scale-calibrated blend (fit g on the labelled judgments of each run) and a strict-aware similarity with a capped logit; none was run (variant budget).

# Update: active selection of pairwise RHEED labels (changes since the Oct 6 report)

- **Where we were (Oct 6).** On 3 seeds, eight strategies were compared by ideal-image type accuracy (28 test images, accuracy 0.4–0.7), and the best strategy changed with the budget (cluster-quota at 10, Cluster-Margin at 25, MC-dropout at 50, and uncertainty+diversity at 100).
- **Problem 1: the numbers were mostly noise.** Several things were wrong. Training changed with the order of the selected pairs. The fine-tuned encoder was unstable. The test set was only 28 images. At budget 100 every strategy received the same 100 groups, because the candidate pool had only 100. I found no code bug. Rankings that looked clear on 5 seeds (e.g. Cluster-Margin, core-set) disappeared on more seeds.
- **Fix 1: a frozen encoder and an order-independent head.** I froze the SimCLR encoder and trained only the reward head in a way that does not depend on pair order, so run-to-run s.d. fell to about 0.04.
- **Problem 2: type accuracy cannot compare strategies.** The ideal-reference anchors alone give 0.848 accuracy with no pair labels. Pair labels alone give 0.37–0.55. A frozen 1-nearest-neighbour baseline gives 0.871. The pair labels teach preference, not class identity.
- **Fix 2: a new endpoint and more strategies.** The endpoint is held-out preference prediction (log-loss and AUC on decisive judgments of image-disjoint pair groups), the same quantity as classifier2's pairwise holdout accuracy. I compared 33 strategy variants, including 17 new ones from the literature (BADGE, TypiClust, Fisher/D-optimal, Laplace-BALD, DPP, ProbCover and others), on 35 random splits, with Wilcoxon tests and Holm correction. I also ran a split that follows classifier2 (20% of pair groups held out).
- **Result.** No strategy is robustly better than random selection; gains are small. Which rule helps depends on the split: Laplace-BALD and BALD×P(decisive) win on the small split with one batch, and only core-set wins on the classifier2-style split. Uncertainty-driven rules never beat random. Coverage rules tend to lead at the smallest budgets.
- **Conflicts with the Oct 6 results.** The per-budget winners above did not replicate on 35 seeds, and the earlier statement that uncertainty works best at budget 100 is not supported. The eight-strategy accuracy table should be read as the "very small budget, noisy endpoint" result, not as a ranking.
- **A flaw in my earlier design: budget and judgments.** The labelling software shows one image pair and one reconstruction type per query, so the true query unit is one (pair, type) judgment. My simulation instead selected a whole pair group and revealed all its judgments (about 3.1 on average), and counted the budget in groups. So "budget 10" actually gave about 31 judgments, and the other types came for free. This applies to every strategy and may have hidden differences between them. I am rerunning the whole comparison with one judgment as the query unit and the budget counted in judgments; results will follow.
